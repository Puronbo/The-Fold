# UF-042 — The explicit five-dimensional reduced quotient

Work over a field k of characteristic two. Let
\[
C=k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2),\qquad
\mathscr A_{\rm red}=H\rtimes B,
\]
where H is the ten-dimensional identity-linear subgroup and
\[
B=\{g(a,b,d):x\mapsto ax+bz,\ z\mapsto dz\},
\quad a,d\in\mathbb G_m,\ b\in\mathbb G_a.
\]
Let N be the eight-dimensional normal subgroup of H defined by the UF-037 equations \(F=0\), \(H=A\). Then
\[
Q=\mathscr A_{\rm red}/N\cong(H/N)\rtimes B.
\]

## 1. The group law of B

Use composition from right to left. Direct substitution gives
\[
\boxed{
(a_1,b_1,d_1)(a_2,b_2,d_2)
=(a_1a_2,\ a_2b_1+b_2d_1,\ d_1d_2).
}
\]
The identity is \((1,0,1)\), and the inverse is
\[
(a,b,d)^{-1}=(a^{-1},b/(ad),d^{-1})
\]
in characteristic two (minus equals plus).

## 2. Coordinates on H/N

The degree-two coefficient map from UF-037 gives
\[
H/N\cong\mathbb G_a^2,
\qquad (F,R),\quad R=H+A.
\]
The group law on this quotient is addition:
\[
(F_1,R_1)+(F_2,R_2)=(F_1+F_2,R_1+R_2).
\]
This follows because composition of identity-linear automorphisms adds their degree-two parts; products of two degree-two perturbations have degree at least three and do not affect these coordinates.

## 3. Theorem UF-042.1 — Explicit B-action

Let an identity-linear automorphism have degree-two part
\[
\delta(x)=A x^2+C xz,\qquad
\delta(z)=F x^2+H xz.
\]
Conjugate by \(g=g(a,b,d)\), where
\[
g(x)=ax+bz,\qquad g(z)=dz.
\]
In characteristic two,
\[
g^{-1}(x)=a^{-1}x+\frac{b}{ad}z,
\qquad g^{-1}(z)=d^{-1}z.
\]
Using \(z^2=0\),
\[
g(x)^2=a^2x^2,\qquad
g(x)g(z)=ad\,xz.
\]
The degree-two part of \(g\delta g^{-1}\) is therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
A'&=aA+\frac{ab}{d}F, &
C'&=dC+bH,\\
F'&=\frac{a^2}{d}F, &
H'&=aH.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, the quotient coordinate \(R=H+A\) transforms as
\[
\boxed{
F'=\frac{a^2}{d}F,\qquad
R'=aR+\frac{ab}{d}F.
}
\]
Thus the action on \(H/N\) is represented, in column coordinates \((F,R)^T\), by
\[
\rho(a,b,d)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2/d&0\\
ab/d&a
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## 4. Theorem UF-042.2 — The semidirect-product law of Q

The preceding formula is a representation of B. Indeed, for the B multiplication in Section 1,
\[
\rho(g_1g_2)=\rho(g_1)\rho(g_2).
\]
Consequently Q has the explicit law
\[
\boxed{
(v_1,g_1)(v_2,g_2)
=
(v_1+\rho(g_1)v_2,\ g_1g_2),
}
\]
where \(v_i=(F_i,R_i)^T\), and
\[
\rho(a,b,d)
\begin{pmatrix}F\\R\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(a^2/d)F\\
(ab/d)F+aR
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is a five-dimensional smooth affine group, not a direct product of \(\mathbb G_a^2\) with B.

## 5. Consequence for UF-041

The quotient map
\[
\mathscr A_{\rm red}\longrightarrow Q
\]
is now explicit on the reduced group. Applying its second Frobenius twist after the second relative Frobenius of the full automorphism scheme gives the canonical morphism
\[
\mathscr A\xrightarrow{F_{\mathscr A}^{(2)}}
\mathscr A_{\rm red}^{(2)}
\longrightarrow Q^{(2)}
\]
from UF-041.

The coordinate law above makes the target and its Frobenius kernel concrete. In particular, the second Frobenius kernel of Q is described by fourth-power equations in five local parameters at the identity. This still does not identify the minimal normal closure: it only gives the explicit ambient upper bound \(\mathscr K\subseteq\ker(\Psi_2)\).
