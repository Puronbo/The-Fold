# UF-048 — Universal conjugation coefficient ideal

## 1. The reduced subgroup and U

Over a field of characteristic two, write
\[
C=k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2).
\]
The reduced identity-linear subgroup H consists of substitutions
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=x+A x^2+B x^3+C_1xz+D x^2z+E x^3z,\\
Z&=z+F x^2+G x^3+H_1xz+I x^2z+J x^3z.
\end{aligned}
\]
The seven-dimensional subgroup U is defined by
\[
F=0,\qquad H_1-A=0,\qquad B+G+I=0.
\]
Thus, within H,
\[
I_H(U)=(F,H_1-A,B+G+I).
\]

For the full automorphism scheme, the same subgroup is embedded with zero constant terms and identity cotangent map. In the notation
\[
X=c+ax+bz+\alpha x^2+\beta x^3+\gamma xz+\delta x^2z+\epsilon x^3z,
\]
\[
Z=d_0+hx+iz+\phi x^2+\psi x^3+\eta xz+\theta x^2z+\kappa x^3z,
\]
its defining ideal is
\[
I_A(U)=
(c,d_0,h,a-1,b,i-1,\phi,\eta-\alpha,\beta+\psi+\theta).
\]
The remaining seven coefficients are free.

## 2. Universal conjugation map

Let G be the full automorphism group scheme and U the subgroup above. The universal conjugation morphism is
\[
\mathrm{Conj}:G\times U\longrightarrow G,
\qquad (g,u)\longmapsto gug^{-1}.
\]
It induces
\[
\mathrm{Conj}^{*}:A\longrightarrow A\otimes_k O(U),
\]
where A=O(G).

For each generator f of I_A(U), expand
\[
\mathrm{Conj}^{*}(f)=\sum_{\nu} f_{\nu}\otimes m_{\nu}
\]
in a k-basis of O(U). The coefficient ideal
\[
J_{\mathrm{orb}}=(f_{\nu}: f\text{ ranges over the displayed generators})
\subseteq A
\]
is the scheme-theoretic ideal of the universal conjugacy orbit family (equivalently, the intersection of the conjugate subgroup ideals, before taking the subgroup generated by that family).

This is the first explicit computational target. It must be computed in the full coordinate ring
\[
A=k[c,d_0,h,a,b,\ldots,\Delta^{-1}]/(c^4,d_0^2,h^2),
\qquad \Delta=ai-bh,
\]
not merely after setting c=d_0=h=0.

## 3. The Hopf-core step

The normal closure is not obtained by simply declaring J_orb to be a Hopf ideal. The subgroup generated by the conjugates is the smallest closed subgroup scheme containing the entire conjugacy family. Equivalently, its defining ideal is the largest conjugation-stable Hopf ideal contained in I_A(U), as formulated in UF-044.

Thus the calculation has two logically separate stages:

1. Compute the coefficient ideal J_orb from the universal conjugation map.
2. Compute the largest Hopf ideal J satisfying
\[
J\subseteq I_A(U),\qquad
\Delta_A(J)\subseteq J\otimes A+A\otimes J,
\]
with the appropriate antipode and counit conditions, and stable under the conjugation coaction.

The second stage is essential. The orbit-family ideal need not itself define a subgroup.

## 4. What is already known

The reduced normal closure is the eight-dimensional subgroup N inside H, with equations F=0 and H_1=A. The full scheme-theoretic normal closure K has reduced support N and tangent dimension 16. The second-Frobenius kernel K_2 is a globally defined normal subgroup scheme containing U, so
\[
K\subseteq K_2.
\]

Neither the equality K=K_2 nor a strict inclusion has been established. Equality of reduced supports and tangent spaces cannot settle the higher-order Hopf ideal.

## 5. A decisive finite-order comparison

Let n=(c,d_0,h) be the nilradical ideal. Since n^4=0, the ambient infinitesimal direction has finite order. The next exact test is to compute the Hopf core modulo
\[
n^2,\quad n^3,\quad n^4=0
\]
and compare it with I(K_2) at each stage.

A discrepancy at the first stage identifies a missing tangent direction (which would contradict the established tangent calculation and therefore also serve as an error check). A discrepancy first appearing modulo n^3 or n^4 would isolate a genuinely higher-order obstruction.

Care is required: the filtration by n is not automatically preserved by every chosen coordinate splitting. The comparison must be made using the intrinsic ideal filtration n^r and the induced quotient maps.

## 6. Current result and open status

UF-048 makes the universal conjugation computation explicit and identifies the exact algebraic stages needed to settle minimality. It does not claim that the coefficient ideal or its Hopf core has been computed. In particular, no equality K=K_2 is asserted.

The next step is to implement the substitution/composition algebra in a symbolic polynomial engine, retain c,d_0,h and impose c^4=d_0^2=h^2=0, then extract coefficients in the seven U-parameters. Only after this full coefficient calculation should the Hopf-core iteration begin.
