# UF-048 — Universal conjugation image and Hopf core

## 1. The subgroup U in full coordinates

Let k have characteristic two and C=k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2). Write a full automorphism as
\[
X=c+ax+bz+\alpha x^2+\beta x^3+\gamma xz+\delta x^2z+\epsilon x^3z,
\]
\[
Z=d_0+hx+iz+\phi x^2+\psi x^3+\eta xz+\theta x^2z+\kappa x^3z.
\]
The full automorphism scheme has c^4=d_0^2=h^2=0 and Delta=ai-bh invertible.

The seven-dimensional subgroup U is embedded by
\[
c=d_0=h=0,\quad a=i=1,\quad b=0,
\]
and
\[
\phi=0,\quad \eta-\alpha=0,\quad \beta+\psi+\theta=0.
\]
Thus
\[
I_A(U)=(c,d_0,h,a-1,b,i-1,\phi,\eta-\alpha,\beta+\psi+\theta).
\]

## 2. The universal conjugacy family

Let G be the full automorphism group scheme. The universal conjugation morphism is
\[
\mathrm{Conj}:G\times U\longrightarrow G,
\qquad (g,u)\longmapsto gug^{-1}.
\]
It induces the algebra map
\[
\mathrm{Conj}^{*}:A\longrightarrow O(G)\otimes_k O(U),
\]
where A=O(G).

The ideal of the scheme-theoretic image of this morphism is
\[
\boxed{J_{\mathrm{orb}}=\ker(\mathrm{Conj}^{*})\subseteq A.}
\]
Equivalently, f lies in J_orb precisely when its pullback vanishes identically in both the conjugating variable g and the subgroup variable u. In a computation, expand the pullback of a general coordinate polynomial f in bases of O(G) and O(U), and impose the vanishing of every coefficient.

It is important not to confuse this with expanding Conj*(f) only for generators f of I_A(U). Such a calculation tests whether gug^{-1} lies in U; it does not compute the ideal of the union of all conjugates. The correct orbit ideal is the kernel above. Since the identity conjugator is included, J_orb is contained in I_A(U).

The computation must retain c,d_0,h and the relations c^4=d_0^2=h^2=0. Restricting to the reduced group first would discard the infinitesimal information sought.

## 3. From the orbit image to the generated subgroup

The scheme-theoretic image of G×U→G is the family of conjugates, not necessarily the subgroup generated by that family. Its ideal J_orb need not be a Hopf ideal.

The normal closure K is the smallest closed subgroup scheme containing that image. On coordinate rings, its ideal is the largest Hopf ideal contained in J_orb. It must satisfy
\[
J\subseteq J_{\mathrm{orb}},\qquad
\Delta_A(J)\subseteq J\otimes A+A\otimes J,
\]
together with the counit and antipode conditions. Since the family is invariant under conjugation, the resulting Hopf ideal is conjugation-stable.

This corrects a common shortcut: the Hopf core must be taken inside the ideal of the universal conjugacy image, not merely inferred from a few coefficient equations of the defining ideal of U.

## 4. Known constraints

The reduced normal closure is the eight-dimensional subgroup N inside the reduced identity-linear group H, with equations F=0 and H_1=A. The full scheme-theoretic normal closure K has reduced support N and tangent dimension 16.

The globally defined second-Frobenius kernel K_2 is a normal subgroup scheme containing U, so
\[
K\subseteq K_2.
\]
The equality K=K_2 remains open. Matching reduced supports and tangent dimensions does not determine higher infinitesimal structure.

## 5. Finite nilradical filtration

Let n=(c,d_0,h) be the nilradical ideal. Every element r of n satisfies r^4=0, but the ideal powers satisfy n^5≠0 and n^6=0. After computing J_orb and its Hopf core, compare the resulting ideal with I(K_2) through the full intrinsic filtration
\[
A\supset n\supset n^2\supset n^3\supset n^4=0.
\]
The first-order comparison must recover the established 16-dimensional tangent space. A discrepancy at order two or three would detect higher-order excess or deficiency in the Frobenius candidate.

The filtration comparison must be carried out intrinsically; a chosen coordinate splitting need not preserve the group structure.
