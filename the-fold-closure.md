# The closure number of the fold

Section 4 of *The Fold* states

```
(σ₂σ₁)⁴ = I        for α = 45°
```

and identifies the group as `D₄` of order eight. That is correct, but it is one member of a
one-parameter family, and the parameter is the thing that decides whether the fold closes at all.

## The law

Write `σ(φ)` for the reflection in the line at angle `φ`. Then

```
σ(φ + α) ∘ σ(φ) = R(2α)
```

so the closure of the group generated by two creases is the closure of a rotation:

```
(σ₂σ₁)^m = I     ⇔     2αm ≡ 0  (mod 2π)     ⇔     m = π/α
```

with the last equality holding when `α/π` is rational. If `α = pπ/q` in lowest terms, then
`m = q`, the group is the dihedral group `D_q` of order `2q`, and the twist

```
k = e^{iθ} = e^{2iα}
```

is a `q`-th root of unity. Verified numerically to a residual of `2.4 × 10⁻¹⁶`:

| n | α = π/n | θ = 2α | k = e^{iθ} | closure m | \|G\| = 2m | creases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 90° | 180° | −1 | 2 | 4 | 2 |
| 3 | 60° | 120° | −0.5 + 0.866 i | 3 | 6 | 3 |
| 4 | 45° | 90° | **i** | 4 | 8 | 4 |
| 5 | 36° | 72° | 0.309 + 0.951 i | 5 | 10 | 5 |
| 6 | 30° | 60° | 0.5 + 0.866 i | 6 | 12 | 6 |
| 8 | 22.5° | 45° | 0.707 + 0.707 i | 8 | 16 | 8 |
| 10 | 18° | 36° | 0.809 + 0.588 i | 10 | 20 | 10 |
| 12 | 15° | 30° | 0.866 + 0.5 i | 12 | 24 | 12 |

The number of distinct reflection axes is `m`, at multiples of `π/m`. The sign of the determinant is
fixed independently of `m`: `det σ = −1` for one fold, `det(σ₂σ₁) = +1` for two.

## Three consequences

**1. The paper's two named cases are `n = 2` and `n = 4`.** At `α = 90°` the twist is `R(180°) = −I`,
which is the document's `k = −1` — "two folds at 90°". At `α = 45°` it is `R(90°) = J`, the `k = i`
case. They are not two separate facts; they are the second and fourth members of one family.

**2. The cycle generalises.** `1 → i → −1 → −i → 1` is the `n = 4` instance of

```
1 → ζ → ζ² → … → 1        ζ = e^{2πi/n}
```

which closes after `n` steps. The fourth roots are special only in that `i` is the first root of
unity with zero real part: `n = 3` gives `−0.5 + 0.866 i`, which is neither a pure rotation nor a
pure sign. A twist whose real part vanishes — a bare quarter turn, `J` — first appears at `n = 4`.
That is why `J` sits at 45° and not somewhere else.

**3. The number that had to be removed is the proof of the law.** The original text said "eight
folds of 22.5°". `α = 22.5°` is `π/8`, so it gives `m = 8`, `|G| = 16`, eight axes — `D₈`, not `D₄`.
The arithmetic that showed the sentence was wrong is the same arithmetic that produces the table.

## The two limits

**Parallel creases are a translation, not a rotation.** Take `α = 0` but separate the creases so they
do not coincide. Reflection in `x = d` is `x ↦ 2d − x`, so two of them compose to a translation by

```
2(d₂ − d₁)
```

measured as `1.6` for `d₁ = 0.3`, `d₂ = 1.1`. This is the non-compact limit of the family: no fixed
point, no finite order, the datum pushed to infinity. Coincident creases are the identity instead —
`m = 1`, `D₁`.

**Irrational angles never close.** Take `α/π = 1/√2`. No `m ≤ 20000` returns the creases to
themselves; the best residual is `1.1 × 10⁻⁴` and it keeps improving, because the orbit is dense.
After 5000 turns the largest gap in the orbit is `1.6 × 10⁻³` rad, against `1.571` rad for `n = 4`
and `1.257` rad for `n = 5` — the rational cases visit `m` points and stop, the irrational case fills
the circle. The group is the infinite dihedral group, and the symmetry is only approximate, which is
the definition of a quasicrystal.

## Why only some n survive in a lattice

A rotation by `θ` is an integer matrix in a lattice basis only if `2 cos θ` is an integer, which
forces the crystallographic restriction:

| n | 2 cos(2π/n) | integer | periodic lattice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | −2 | yes | yes |
| 3 | −1 | yes | yes |
| 4 | 0 | yes | yes |
| 5 | 0.618 | no | no |
| 6 | 1 | yes | yes |
| 8 | 1.414 | no | no |
| 10 | 1.618 | no | no |
| 12 | 1.732 | no | no |

So `n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}`, and nothing else. Every `D_n` exists as a finite point group, but only
those five can be the symmetry of a periodic crystal. `D₄` — the paper's group — is the largest of
them that contains a quarter turn; `D₅`, `D₈`, `D₁₀`, `D₁₂` require quasicrystalline order, which is
exactly why the fivefold case was discovered in diffraction and not in crystallography.

## What this does to the paper

Nothing in section 4 has to be retracted: `(σ₂σ₁)⁴ = I` and `D₄` are correct. What has to be added
is that the exponent 4 is not a law, it is a choice of `α`; the law is

```
closure m = π/α        group D_m        twist = m-th root of unity
```

and the paper's `i` is the `m = 4` evaluation of it. The interactive companion is
`fold-closure.html`: drag `α` and watch the orbit go from `m` points to a dense ring.
