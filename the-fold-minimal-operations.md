# The minimal operation family

## The question

The Fold has more operations than it needs. Scale, fold, twist, doubling, cut, cap, negation — each is
a different map, and the corpus has never said which of them are forced. This note answers the question
recorded as UF-002: how few operations preserve a chosen amount of information without making the
invariant trivial, so that the core transformations can be selected on principle?

The answer is not a number. It is a test. **Every amount of information costs at most one scale
exactly when the unit group is cyclic.** The fold does not enter the count as one more operation: it is
the parity of the information, and the parity decides which half of the character lattice a family can
preserve.

## The host

The finite host is `ℤ/n` with its unit group `U(n)`. A **scale** is an element `a ∈ U(n)`, acting by
`x ↦ ax`; the **fold** is `a = −1`; **doubling** is `a = 2`, which is a scale only when `n` is odd.
A **family** `F ⊆ U(n)` generates a subgroup `H = ⟨F⟩`, and the information `H` preserves is the
algebra of functions constant on its orbits.

On a finite abelian group that algebra is generated by the characters of `U(n)/H` — equivalently, by
the characters `χ` of `U(n)` with `H ⊆ ker χ`. So one piece of information is a character, and the
family that preserves it is exactly `ker χ`. There are two extremes:

- `χ = 1`, the constant: every scale preserves it, and it carries nothing.
- `χ` faithful, `ker χ = 1`: only the identity preserves it, and it carries the group element itself.

Define the **cost** `d(χ)` as the least number of scales that generate `ker χ`. This is the quantity
UF-002 asks for, and it is computable: the tool `tools/minimal_operations.py` enumerates the
characters, generates each kernel, and searches for the smallest generating set.

| 8 | 4 | 2+2 | 2 | 1:3, 2:1 | 0 |

| 27 | 18 | 18 | 1 | 0:6, 1:12 | 6 |
| 45 | 24 | 6+4 | 2 | 1:18, 2:6 | 0 |

The dividing line is not the size of `n`; it is the shape of `U(n)`. The moduli `5, 7, 9, 11, 25, 27`
have cyclic unit groups — they are `p^k` or `2p^k` — and every cost there is `0` or `1`. The moduli
`8, 15, 16, 21, 45` do not, and each of them has a cost `2` and no cost `0`. Three exact statements
explain the table.

1. **Cost zero is faithfulness.** A finite subgroup of `ℂ*` is cyclic, so `U(n)` has a faithful
   character if and only if `U(n)` is cyclic. When it exists, the information is the group element and
   only the identity preserves it.
2. **Cost one is cyclicity, the constant included.** Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic, so if
   `U(n)` is cyclic then `d(χ) ≤ 1` for every `χ`. Conversely, take `χ = 1`: its kernel is all of
   `U(n)`, so `d(1) ≤ 1` forces `U(n)` to be cyclic. Hence *every piece of information costs at most
   one scale if and only if `U(n)` is cyclic* — where "every" includes the coarsest information. Exclude
   the constant as trivial and the converse fails: `n = 8` has `U(8) ≅ C₂ × C₂`, not cyclic, and every
   nontrivial character has kernel `C₂`, so every proper level costs one. What a non-cyclic unit group
   cannot avoid is a level above one; `C₂ × C₂` has it only at the top.
3. **The maximum is the rank.** `d` is maximised by `χ = 1`, where it is the number of cyclic factors
   in the CRT decomposition. That number is unbounded: `n = 840 = 2³·3·5·7` has rank 5, and
   `n = 510510 = 2·3·5·7·11·13·17` has rank 6.

The exact form of the dividing line is therefore
`max_χ d(χ) = rank U(n)`, and that maximum is at most `1` exactly when `U(n)` is cyclic. On a cyclic
group one operation is always enough and no level needs two; everywhere else a level that needs two
appears. It is the coarsest level: the family that preserves the constant function is the whole unit
group, so asking for no information costs the most.

## The correction

The obvious formula is `d(χ) =` the number of cyclic factors on which `χ` is not faithful. It is wrong,
and the error is worth keeping because it is the natural one. The kernel of a tensor product is not the
product of the kernels: it is one diagonal congruence. On `n = 15`, the character `j = (1,1)` is
faithful on the `C₂` factor and on the `C₄` factor, and its kernel is still `{1, 14}` — the two faithful
factors cancel at `14`, because `(−1)·(−1) = 1`. Its cost is 1, not 0, and the naive count would have
called it free.

The exact form is a congruence. Write `L = lcm(m₁, …, m_k)` for the factor orders, `c_i = j_i L/m_i`,
and `d_i(x)` for the exponent of `x` in the `i`-th cyclic factor. Then

```
χ(x) = ζ_L^{Σ c_i d_i(x)}          χ(x) = 1  ⟺  Σ c_i d_i(x) ≡ 0 (mod L)
```

so `ker χ` is the kernel of a single map to `ℤ/L`, not a product. Its order is `|U(n)| / ord(χ)`, and
its rank is the rank of that congruence subgroup. That is what the tool computes, by generating the
kernel in `U(n)` and searching.

## The fold is the parity

Since `(−1)² = 1`, every character takes `−1` to `±1`, and the fold preserves `χ` exactly when
`χ(−1) = 1`. The even characters are the annihilator of `⟨−1⟩`, an index-2 subgroup of the character
group for every `n > 2`: **exactly half the character lattice is preserved by the fold, and the other
half is what detects it.**

Two consequences, both exact.

- **An even character never costs zero.** A faithful character has `ker χ = 1`, but `−1 ∈ ker χ` for
  every even character, and `−1 ≠ 1`. So the free information is always odd, and every piece of
  information the fold preserves costs at least one scale.
- **On a cyclic unit group, an even character costs exactly one.** It is not faithful, so its kernel is
  a proper nontrivial subgroup of a cyclic group, hence cyclic. The fold's own information therefore
  sits at cost 1: never zero, never two.

The cheapest information is not always the fold's, and on `n = 15` the six cost-1 characters split into
four odd and two even. The cheapest odd one has kernel `{1, 11}`, so its blocks are

```
[1, 11]   [2, 7]   [4, 14]   [8, 13]
```

and its preserving family is `{1, 11}`; the fold is not in it. The fold sends `[1, 11]` to `[4, 14]`:
it preserves the partition as a set of blocks and exchanges the blocks, which is exactly what
`χ(−1) = −1` says. Two readings of "the same information" diverge here. As a character, the information
is destroyed by the fold; as a partition, it is preserved and relabelled. In general the fold permutes
the cosets of `ker χ` for every `χ`, and fixes each coset exactly when `χ` is even. **The cost counts the
subgroup, and the fold acts on the labelling.** A two-block partition with an exchanged pair is a
`ℤ/2`-torsor, and the fold is its monodromy. That sentence is a reformulation: it names the cover and
proves nothing about the Fold.

## Doubling's shifted period

Doubling is the one scale the corpus already carries, and the Lean library now has both period sets,
for `n > 1`:

```
iterate (double n) k = id          ⟺   n ∣ 2^k − 1
iterate (double n) k 1 = −1        ⟺   n ∣ 2^k + 1
```

For `n > 2` the two are disjoint: a common `k` would make `n ∣ 2`. So **a scale cannot be at the
identity and at the fold at the same time.** Both statements are exact, and both are in `Fold/Doubling.lean`.

In the cyclic cases the fold's period exists exactly when the order of `2` is even; the table adds two
non-cyclic moduli with even order to show the failure mode:

| `n` | `ord_n(2)` | `k` with `2^k ≡ −1` |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 5 | 4 | 2 |
| 7 | 3 | none |
| 9 | 6 | 3 |
| 11 | 10 | 5 |
| 13 | 12 | 6 |
| 15 | 4 | none |
| 21 | 6 | none |
| 25 | 20 | 10 |
| 27 | 18 | 9 |

For odd `n` with cyclic `U(n)` — the odd prime powers `p^e` — `−1` is the only element of order 2, and
the parity of the order is the whole test. `n = 7` fails because the order is odd. `n = 15` and `n = 21`
fail *with* even order: their unit groups have several elements of order 2, and the unique order-2
element of `⟨2⟩` is not `−1`.

It is tempting to conclude that the same hypothesis decides both questions — the cost of information
and the fold's period. It does not, and the counterexamples are exact. `n = 33 = 3·11` has
`U(33) ≅ C₂ × C₁₀`, not cyclic, yet `ord_33(2) = 10` and `2⁵ = 32 ≡ −1`. `n = 65 = 5·13` has
`U(65) ≅ C₄ × C₁₂`, not cyclic, yet `ord_65(2) = 12` and `2⁶ = 64 ≡ −1`. So reaching the fold is a
statement about `⟨2⟩`, not about cyclicity. Conversely `n = 7` is cyclic and never reaches it. The
tempting identification of the two tests is a rhyme, and it is a false one: the two are independent.

What survives is one implication, exact but one-directional:

> for odd `n`: `U(n)` cyclic ⟹ (doubling reaches the fold ⟺ `ord_n(2)` is even).

The counting question has its own exact answer, `U(n)` cyclic ⟺ every information costs at most one
scale, and that is the principle on which the operations can be selected: not minimality and not
geometry, but the cyclicity of an arithmetic group. The fold's period answers a different question —
whether `−1` happens to lie in the cyclic subgroup the scale generates — and it is decided by the
arithmetic of `2`, not by the shape of `U(n)`.

## The zones

| claim | zone | what it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| the cost of a character is the least number of generators of its kernel | exact | subgroups of a finite abelian group, and the character lattice |
| every information costs at most one scale iff `U(n)` is cyclic | exact | subgroups of a cyclic group are cyclic; the constant's kernel is the whole group |
| an even character costs at least one scale, and on a cyclic unit group exactly one | exact | `−1 ∈ ker χ`, and a faithful character is odd |
| the fold's exchange of two blocks is the monodromy of a `ℤ/2`-torsor | reformulation | the cover is named; this is not a derivation |
| a minimal operation family is a complete set of commuting observables | rhyme | the two are sets of operators with a joint eigenbasis, but the objects differ; this is not a derivation |
| irreducible means "needs one generator" | false friend | irreducibility is about invariant subspaces, not about the size of a generating set |
| the cost test and the fold-period test have the same hypothesis | false friend | `n = 33` and `n = 65` are not cyclic and do reach the fold, so the two answers come apart |

## What is missing

Three absences, named rather than hidden.

**A measure of information.** "Amount" is not defined. The character lattice gives a partial order —
finer information is a smaller kernel — and the cost is a function on that lattice, not on a number.
The question "how much information" is therefore only as sharp as the choice of character.

**A principle selecting `−1`.** The fold is selected by parity, not by minimality. On the host it is
the involution `x ↦ −x`; on a cyclic unit group it is the only nontrivial one, while on a non-cyclic
group it is one of several (`U(8) ≅ C₂ × C₂` has three). That the fold is this involution is a fact
about the host, not a derivation; the parity test then says which characters survive it.

**The infinite host.** On the positive scale group `ℝ_{>0}` the nontrivial characters are `x ↦ x^s`
for real `s ≠ 0`, all faithful, so no proper kernel has to be generated and every nontrivial level
costs zero. The fold acts there as `s ↦ −s`, on the labelling: the continuous analogue of parity. The
finite count is a finite-group count, and the continuous question — the number of generators of a
one-parameter group — has a different answer, zero, for a different reason.

## The finding in one sentence

**One operation is enough exactly when the unit group is cyclic, and the fold is never that operation:
it is the parity of the information, it fixes exactly half the character lattice, and the information
it fixes is never free.**
