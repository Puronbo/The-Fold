# What is invariant

Everything in the paper moves under `k`. The question "what's" is the question of what does not.

There are three answers, one per group you are willing to allow.

## Orbits

| group | orbits on `ℝⁿ ∖ {P₀}` | quotient | invariant |
|---|---|---|---|
| `g_k`, `k > 0` | open rays from the datum | `Sⁿ⁻¹` | direction, ratios |
| `g_k`, `k ≠ 0` | lines through the datum | `ℝPⁿ⁻¹` | direction up to sign |
| all folds `O(n)` through the datum | spheres about the datum | `ℝ₊` | radius, all lengths |
| both together (similarities fixing `P₀`) | **everything** | a point | constants only |

The last row is the sharp one. Folds alone already act transitively on each sphere; scalings then
carry one sphere to any other; so the two together move any point of `ℝⁿ ∖ {P₀}` to any other. The
generated group is `ℝ₊ × O(n)`, it is transitive, and its invariant ring is `ℝ`.

The two columns meet only in `{I, −I}`, since `kI` is orthogonal iff `|k| = 1`.

## The audit

Measured on a fixed triangle, at `k = 1.85` and a plane of normal `n = (0.48, 0.60, −0.64)/|·|`:

| quantity | scaling | fold |
|---|---|---|
| pairwise distances | ✗ rel. change `0.850` | ✓ `0.0` |
| radius from `P₀` | ✗ rel. change `0.850` | ✓ `2.2e−16` |
| direction from `P₀` | ✓ `0.0°` | ✗ `87.0°` |
| vertex angles | ✓ `1.3e−14°` | ✓ `1.3e−14°` |
| cross-ratio | ✓ `2.2e−16` | ✓ `0.0` |

The zeros in the second column are exact, not small: a reflection is an isometry. The `0.850` in the
first column is exactly `|k − 1| = 0.85` — a scaling multiplies every length by `k`, so the relative
change is the same for all of them.

Angles and the cross-ratio survive both. They are the only nontrivial quantities that do, and they
survive for the same reason: both maps are affine, and the cross-ratio is the affine invariant of
four collinear points. A similarity is conformal, so angles never had a chance to fail.

## Where the columns meet: `k = −1`

At `k = −1` the scaling row reads like the fold row:

| quantity | `k = −1` |
|---|---|
| pairwise distances | ✓ `0.0` |
| radius from `P₀` | ✓ `0.0` |
| direction from `P₀` | ✗ `180.00°` |
| vertex angles | ✓ `0.0°` |
| cross-ratio | ✓ `0.0e+0` |

It is an isometry. The one thing it does not preserve is the *ray*: every direction is reversed. So
what survives is the direction as a **line**, and that is why the quotient for `k ∈ ℝ*` is `ℝPⁿ⁻¹`
rather than `Sⁿ⁻¹`. The sign of `k` *is* the antipodal identification, and `k = −1` is the map that
performs it. The fold's collapse is the generator of the projective quotient.

## Polynomial invariants

Under `P ↦ kP` a monomial of degree `d` is multiplied by `k^d`. Invariance for every `k` therefore
forces `d = 0`:

| degree | `|m(kP) − m(P)|` at `k = 1.7` |
|---|---|
| 0 | `0.0` |
| 1 | `0.770` |
| 2 | `2.287` |
| 3 | `5.208` |

So the **polynomial** invariant ring of the scaling group is the constants, even though the
**rational** invariants are plentiful (`x_i/x_j`, and the cross-ratio among them). This is the
difference between a ring and a field, and it is why the paper's invariants are all ratios.

The fold is the opposite: `n·P` flips sign, but

```
(n·P)²  invariant      err = 4.9e−17
P − (n·P)n  invariant  err = 1.6e−16
```

so its ring is generated by `(n·P)²` together with the tangential coordinates — a large ring, because
a reflection is a finite group of order two.

## The division family's frozen cross-ratio

The previous thread showed that the division points all move as `k` changes. Their cross-ratio does
not. With `P` at position `1`, `g_kP` at `k`, and `q_t`, `q_s` at `1 + t(k−1)`, `1 + s(k−1)`:

```
CR(P, g_kP; q_t, q_s) = t(1 − s) / (s(1 − t))
```

The `(k − 1)` cancels from numerator and denominator. Measured at `t = ½`, `s = ⅓`, where the
formula gives `2`:

| `k` | `−3` | `−1` | `0` | `0.5` | `2` | `4` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CR | `2.000000000000` | `2.000000000000` | `2.000000000000` | `2.000000000000` | `2.000000000000` | `2.000000000000` |

Max deviation `4.4e−16`; the case `k = 1` is excluded because all four points coincide and the
cross-ratio is undefined. So the evolution moves the points but not the projective configuration —
the one-parameter family is a rigid motion of the projective line.

## At `φ = 0`

At `k = 0`, or at the collapse value of any division point, rank drops to zero and every row of the
audit becomes undefined rather than false. There is no distance to compare, no angle to measure, no
cross-ratio: four points have become one. The invariant ring is the constants, and the orbit space is
a single point — which is the invariant-theoretic reading of the paper's "zero as the point".

## The three sentences

- **Scaling** keeps direction and cross-ratio, destroys length.
- **Folding** keeps length and angle, destroys direction.
- **Both** keep nothing but the datum and the dimension.

The fold sits at the hinge of that table because it is the only isometry in the scaling column, and
`k = −1` is the only map that is both.
