# UF-043 — The explicit Hopf ideal and size of the second-Frobenius candidate

Work over a perfect field k of characteristic two. Write the five quotient coordinates of UF-042 as
\[
(a,b,d;F,R),\qquad a,d\in\mathbb G_m,\quad b,F,R\in\mathbb G_a,
\]
with identity \((1,0,1;0,0)\), and group law
\[
(v_1,g_1)(v_2,g_2)=(v_1+\rho(g_1)v_2,g_1g_2),
\quad
\rho(a,b,d)=
\begin{pmatrix}a^2/d&0\\ab/d&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(Q\) denote this smooth five-dimensional group.

## 1. Theorem UF-043.1 — Global Hopf ideal of the second Frobenius kernel

The second relative Frobenius
\[
F_Q^{(2)}:Q\longrightarrow Q^{(2)}
\]
has coordinate pullback given by fourth powers (with the usual twist on scalars). Its kernel is the fiber over the identity of \(Q^{(2)}\). Therefore its ideal in
\[
k[Q]=k[a,a^{-1},d,d^{-1},b,F,R]
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
I(Q_2)=
(a^4-1,\ b^4,\ d^4-1,\ F^4,\ R^4).
}
\]
In characteristic two, \(a^4-1=(a-1)^4\) and \(d^4-1=(d-1)^4\). Thus this is a global presentation, not only a local-parameter description.

It is a Hopf ideal because it is the kernel of a group-scheme homomorphism. This also avoids the invalid shortcut of declaring arbitrary fourth powers of affine coordinates to form a Hopf ideal without checking the group law.

## 2. Length of the quotient-side Frobenius kernel

The coordinate algebra is
\[
k[Q_2]\cong
k[a,a^{-1},d,d^{-1},b,F,R]/
(a^4-1,b^4,d^4-1,F^4,R^4).
\]
Since the relations force a=1 and d=1 up to fourth-order infinitesimals, the inverses introduce no additional length. The five truncated parameters each have length four. Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{length}(Q_2)=4^5=1024.}
\]

## 3. Pullback ideal inside the full automorphism scheme

Use the full automorphism coordinates from UF-033:
\[
X=c+a x+bz+c_2x^2+c_3x^3+c_{11}xz+c_{21}x^2z+c_{31}x^3z,
\]
\[
Z=e+h x+i z+e_2x^2+e_3x^3+e_{11}xz+e_{21}x^2z+e_{31}x^3z,
\]
where \(c^4=e^2=h^2=0\) and \(ai-bh\) is invertible. (The letter e here is the constant coefficient of Z; it is distinct from the graded parameter d used above.)

On the reduced locus, the quotient map \(\mathscr A_{\rm red}\to Q\) records the graded parameters \((a,b,i)\) and the two quotient coordinates \((F,R)\), where F and R are the UF-037 degree-two combinations. Its composite with the second relative Frobenius has pullback generated by the fourth powers of these five functions.

Consequently the candidate \(\mathscr K_2\) has ideal
\[
\boxed{
I(\mathscr K_2)=
(c^4,e^2,h^2,\ a^4-1,\ b^4,\ i^4-1,\ F^4,\ R^4)
}
\]
inside the ambient coordinate ring, with F and R interpreted as the actual reduced quotient functions in the full automorphism coordinate presentation. The equations \(c^4=e^2=h^2=0\) are already ambient relations, so the new kernel equations are precisely the five fourth-power quotient equations.

This is the explicit pullback Hopf ideal description of the UF-041 candidate. It is important that F and R are the quotient functions, not arbitrary individual coefficients chosen without the quotient map.

## 4. Length over the reduced normal subgroup

The reduced quotient map \(\mathscr A_{\rm red}\to Q\) is an N-torsor. In the explicit affine coordinates from UF-037 and UF-042, the eight coordinates along N are free and the five transverse quotient coordinates are the five parameters above. Thus the second-Frobenius fiber has rank 1024 over N.

The full automorphism scheme has, independently, the three nilpotent coordinates with lengths 4, 2, and 2, giving a factor
\[
4\cdot2\cdot2=16.
\]
Accordingly the candidate \(\mathscr K_2\) is finite locally free of rank
\[
\boxed{1024\cdot16=16384}
\]
over N, provided the stated global N-torsor coordinate splitting is used. Equivalently its total dimension is eight and its coordinate algebra has generic length 16384 over its reduced support.

This length is for the explicit upper-bound candidate \(\mathscr K_2\), not for the minimal normal closure \(\mathscr K\).

## 5. What this does and does not settle

We now have an explicit global Hopf ideal for \(\mathscr K_2\), and an explicit length prediction. The minimal scheme-theoretic normal closure satisfies
\[
\mathscr K\subseteq\mathscr K_2.
\]
The inclusion may be strict. The tangent-space result from UF-039 cannot distinguish these possibilities.

**Still open:** compute the Hopf ideal generated by all universal scheme-valued conjugates of U, and compare it with \(I(\mathscr K_2)\). This is the exact minimality problem.
