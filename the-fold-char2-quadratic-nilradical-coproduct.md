# UF-051 — Constant-term coproduct and the full quadratic nilradical layer

## 1. Correct degree-two basis

The ambient nilradical is
\[
\mathfrak n=(c,d_0,h),\qquad c^4=d_0^2=h^2=0.
\]
The degree-two monomial basis is
\[
\boxed{c^2,\;cd_0,\;ch,\;d_0h.}
\]
The two other quadratic monomials vanish by the defining relations. Thus the rank-four statement in UF-049/050 has four explicit generators; it is not only the three mixed products. This corrects the narrower initial target stated at the end of UF-050.

## 2. Composition convention and constant terms

Use right-to-left composition: for automorphisms g and g', the composite g∘g' is obtained by substituting the images X',Z' of g' into the coordinate polynomials X,Z of g.

Write
\[
\begin{aligned}
X={}&c+ax+bz+\alpha x^2+\beta x^3+\gamma xz
       +\delta x^2z+\epsilon x^3z,\\
Z={}&d_0+hx+iz+\phi x^2+\psi x^3+\eta xz
       +\theta x^2z+\kappa x^3z,
\end{aligned}
\]
and use primes for the coefficients of g'. Evaluating the outer coordinate polynomials at the constant terms c',d₀' gives the constant coefficients of the composite:
\[
\begin{aligned}
c(g\circ g')={}&c+ac'+bd_0'
 +\alpha(c')^2+\beta(c')^3
 +\gamma c'd_0'+\delta(c')^2d_0'
 +\epsilon(c')^3d_0',\\
d_0(g\circ g')={}&d_0+hc'+id_0'
 +\phi(c')^2+\psi(c')^3
 +\eta c'd_0'+\theta(c')^2d_0'
 +\kappa(c')^3d_0'.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are exact polynomial identities in the coordinate ring; terms are interpreted subject to (c')⁴=(d₀')²=(h')²=0.

The coefficient of x in the composite Z(X',Z') is
\[
\boxed{h(g\circ g')=ha'+ih'+\eta(c'h'+d_0'a').}
\]
Indeed, only hX', iZ', and ηX'Z' can contribute an x-linear term. The x-linear coefficient of X'Z' is c'h'+d₀'a'; all square and higher-degree terms have no x-linear component in characteristic two.

These formulas determine the coproducts of the three nilpotent coordinate generators c,d₀,h, and hence the induced group-law filtration data needed for the nilradical comparison.

## 3. Associated-graded degree two

The associated-graded quadratic piece is free of rank four over the reduced coefficient base:
\[
\operatorname{gr}^2_{\mathfrak n}A
=\langle c^2,cd_0,ch,d_0h\rangle.
\]
For any Hopf ideal comparison, the induced coaction must be computed on all four generators. In particular, the square c² cannot be omitted merely because it is not a mixed product.

The displayed coproduct formulas are a starting point, not yet the full conjugation coaction: to obtain conjugation one must combine coproduct, antipode, and multiplication. No claim is made here that any of the four quadratic classes belongs to the normal-closure ideal.
