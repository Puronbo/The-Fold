# UF-039 — The infinitesimal normal closure in characteristic two

Work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic two, with
\[
C=k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2).
\]
Let \(\mathscr A=\operatorname{Aut}(C)\) be the full automorphism group scheme, let \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}\) be its reduced subgroup, and retain \(U\subset N\subset H\) from UF-037 and UF-038.

## 1. The three genuinely non-smooth directions

UF-033 gives the full group-scheme equations for the images of x and z. The three nilpotent constant/linear coefficients satisfy
\[
c^4=d^2=h^2=0.
\]
The reduced locus has \(c=d=h=0\), and its dimension is 13; the tangent space at the identity has dimension 16.

The six directions outside \(\operatorname{Lie}(H)\) split as follows:
- three are genuine tangent directions of the reduced graded subgroup, whose cotangent matrices are upper triangular;
- three are the infinitesimal directions represented by the coefficients \(c,d,h\), which vanish on every field-valued point.

Thus
\[
\dim \mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}=13,
\qquad
\dim T_1\mathscr A=16,
\qquad
\operatorname{edim}_1(\mathscr A)-\dim_1(\mathscr A)=3.
\]
The scheme is non-smooth by exactly three tangent dimensions.

## 2. The reduced normal closure remains N

The reduced group has a split description
\[
\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}=H\rtimes B,
\]
where \(H\cong\mathbb A^{10}\) is the identity-linear kernel and
\[
B=\left\{x\mapsto ax+bz,\ z\mapsto dz: a,d\in k^\times,\ b\in k\right\}
\]
is the three-dimensional upper-triangular graded subgroup.

Recall that N is defined inside H by the two quadratic-coefficient equations \(F=0\) and \(H=A\). It is normal in H by UF-037. It is also stable under conjugation by B. To see this, identify its degree-two tangent data with
\[
\delta_{A,C}(x)=Ax^2+Cxz,
\qquad
\delta_{A,C}(z)=Axz.
\]
For \(g(x)=ax+bz,\ g(z)=dz\), direct conjugation gives
\[
A'=aA,\qquad C'=dC+bA,
\]
so the defining relation between the xz coefficient of the second component and the x² coefficient of the first is preserved. All higher-degree coefficients are unrestricted. Hence N is normal in the full reduced group.

Since the normal closure of U inside H is N (UF-037), the normal closure of U inside \(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}\) is still exactly N.

## 3. The full Lie algebra is generated by infinitesimal conjugation

In characteristic two every pair \((P,Q)\in C\oplus C\) defines a derivation
\[
D=P\partial_x+Q\partial_z.
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr A)=\operatorname{Der}_k(C)\) has dimension 16.

The Lie algebra of U contains the following three elements:
\[
\delta_A=x^2\partial_x+xz\partial_z,
\quad
\delta_B=x^3\partial_x+x^2z\partial_z,
\quad
\delta_C=xz\partial_x.
\]
Let \(e_x=\partial_x\) and \(e_z=\partial_z\). The following brackets are exact in C:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
[e_x,\delta_A]=z\partial_z,
&[e_z,\delta_A]=x\partial_z,\\
[e_x,\delta_B]=x^2\partial_x,
&[e_z,\delta_B]=x^2\partial_z,\\
[e_x,\delta_C]=z\partial_x,
&[e_z,\delta_C]=x\partial_x,\\
[e_z,z\partial_z]=\partial_z,
&[e_z,z\partial_x]=\partial_x.
\end{array}
\]
Together with the seven-dimensional \(\operatorname{Lie}(U)\), these elements span all sixteen basis derivations \(x^iz^j\partial_x\) and \(x^iz^j\partial_z\), for \(0\le i\le3,\ 0\le j\le1\).

It follows that the smallest Lie ideal of \(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr A)\) containing \(\operatorname{Lie}(U)\) is the entire derivation algebra:
\[
\boxed{
\left\langle\operatorname{Lie}(U)\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Lie\ ideal}}
=\operatorname{Der}_k(C).
}
\]
Equivalently, infinitesimal conjugation by the three non-reduced tangent directions, together with further brackets, generates every tangent direction of the full automorphism scheme.

## 4. The scheme-theoretic normal closure: what is proved

Let \(\mathscr K\) denote the scheme-theoretic normal closure of U in \(\mathscr A\), defined as the smallest closed normal subgroup scheme containing U (or equivalently the intersection of all such subgroup schemes).

The reduced support of \(\mathscr K\) is N: non-reduced conjugating points have identity as their underlying field-valued point, while the reduced normal closure was shown above to be N. On the other hand, the Lie-ideal calculation forces
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr K)=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr A),
\qquad \dim T_1\mathscr K=16.}
\]
Thus \(\mathscr K\) is a non-reduced thickening supported on the eight-dimensional subgroup N, with a sixteen-dimensional tangent space at the identity.

This is the key new distinction:
\[
\boxed{
(\mathscr K)_{\mathrm{red}}=N\quad(\dim=8),
\qquad
\dim T_1\mathscr K=16.
}
\]
It is not justified to identify \(\mathscr K\) with all of \(\mathscr A\): equal tangent spaces do not determine a finite-type non-reduced group scheme. Determining the exact defining Hopf ideal and nilpotence orders of this thickening remains open.

## 5. Consequences and next problem

The reduced normal closure is small (dimension eight), but the scheme-theoretic normal closure detects all sixteen infinitesimal directions. This is a concrete mechanism by which the characteristic-two automorphism scheme remembers substantially more than its field-valued points.

**UF-040:** compute the Hopf ideal of \(\mathscr K\) inside the coordinate ring of \(\mathscr A\). In particular, determine whether the three ambient nilpotent parameters \(c,d,h\) survive independently in \(\mathscr K\), or whether conjugation imposes additional nilpotence or mixed relations. Compare its length/thickening structure with the ambient equations \(c^4=d^2=h^2=0\).
