# UF-040 — Frobenius-kernel candidate for the infinitesimal normal closure

Work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic two. Let
\[
C=k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2),\qquad \mathscr A=\operatorname{Aut}(C),
\]
and retain the subgroups \(U\subset N\subset H\) from UF-037–UF-039.

## 1. The quotient visible on reduced points

UF-039 establishes
\[
\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}=H\rtimes B,
\qquad \dim H=10,\quad \dim B=3,
\]
and that N is normal in the full reduced group, with \(\dim N=8\). Consequently the reduced quotient
\[
Q:=\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}/N
\]
is a smooth five-dimensional algebraic group:
\[
\boxed{\dim Q=13-8=5.}
\]

As a variety, its five parameters consist of the three graded parameters in B and the two coordinates of \(H/N\cong\mathbb G_a^2\). The group law is a semidirect-product law determined by the B-action on \(H/N\); it is not in general the direct-product law.

## 2. Candidate theorem: the normal closure is a Frobenius preimage

Let
\[
F_Q:Q\longrightarrow Q^{(1)}
\]
be the relative Frobenius morphism. Its kernel \(Q_1\), the first Frobenius kernel, is a finite infinitesimal normal subgroup scheme supported at the identity. Since Q is smooth of dimension five,
\[
\dim_k\operatorname{Lie}(Q_1)=5.
\]

The natural candidate is
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K_{\mathrm{cand}}
=
\ker\left(
\mathscr A\xrightarrow{\,\pi\,}Q
\xrightarrow{\,F_Q\,}Q^{(1)}
\right),
}
\]
where \(\pi\) would have to be an extension to the full non-reduced group scheme of the reduced quotient map
\(\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}\to Q\).

If such a group-scheme morphism \(\pi\) exists and is suitably faithfully flat, then the candidate has the predicted properties:
- its reduced support is N, because the Frobenius kernel of a smooth connected group is supported at the identity;
- its reduced dimension is eight;
- the differential of relative Frobenius is zero, so the tangent space of the composite kernel is all of \(T_1\mathscr A\), of dimension sixteen.

This matches the two independent constraints from UF-039:
\[
(\mathscr K_{\mathrm{cand}})_{\mathrm{red}}=N,
\qquad
\dim T_1\mathscr K_{\mathrm{cand}}=16.
\]

## 3. Why this is not yet a proof

There are two substantial gaps.

**Extension gap.** The quotient \(\pi:\mathscr A_{\mathrm{red}}\to Q\) is defined on the reduced group. The full automorphism scheme has nilpotent parameters \(c,d,h\), with
\[
c^4=d^2=h^2=0.
\]
One must construct a morphism from the full scheme \(\mathscr A\) to Q (or directly to \(Q^{(1)}\)) compatible with multiplication. Simply discarding the nilpotent parameters is not automatically a group homomorphism.

**Minimality gap.** Even if the candidate is a normal subgroup scheme containing U and has the right reduced support and tangent space, these facts alone do not prove that it is the *smallest* such subgroup scheme. Distinct non-reduced subgroup schemes can have the same reduced support and the same tangent space. One must show that conjugates of U generate the entire Frobenius-kernel thickening, including its higher-order structure.

## 4. Coordinate-level target

On the reduced subgroup, write an element as a graded element of B followed by an element of H. The quotient coordinates can be taken as
\[
(a,b,d;F,R),
\]
where \(a,d\in\mathbb G_m\), \(b,F,R\in\mathbb G_a\), and \((F,R)\) are the two coordinates of \(H/N\). The five coordinates are not all additive: the B-action twists the last two.

For a smooth group Q, the first Frobenius kernel is cut out locally at the identity by the squares of five regular parameters. Thus, after choosing suitable local coordinates \(t_1,\ldots,t_5\) at the identity, its ideal is
\[
(t_1^2,t_2^2,t_3^2,t_4^2,t_5^2).
\]
This is a local description; a global Hopf-ideal presentation must use the actual group law and coordinate functions, not merely square arbitrary affine coordinates.

The full ambient nilpotent coefficients \(c,d,h\) must also be tracked in any proposed extension. They cannot be treated as independent additive coordinates without checking the multiplication law.

## 5. UF-040 research tasks

1. Compute the explicit semidirect-product law of Q in the coordinates \((a,b,d;F,R)\).
2. Determine whether the reduced quotient map extends to a homomorphism from \(\mathscr A\), or whether a different morphism to \(Q^{(1)}\) can be constructed.
3. If an extension exists, compute its kernel's Hopf ideal in the full coordinate ring, including all mixed terms involving \(c,d,h\).
4. Compute the subgroup scheme generated by the conjugates of U over dual numbers and then over higher nilpotent test algebras. This is needed to prove minimality, not merely equality of tangent spaces.
5. Compare the resulting Hopf ideal with the pullback of the first Frobenius-kernel ideal of Q.

## 6. Current status

**Proved in UF-037–UF-039:** the reduced normal closure is N; the Lie ideal generated by \(\operatorname{Lie}(U)\) in the full derivation algebra is all sixteen dimensions.

**New structural candidate in UF-040:** the scheme-theoretic normal closure may be the preimage of the first Frobenius kernel of the five-dimensional reduced quotient Q.

**Open:** construction of the quotient morphism on the full non-reduced automorphism scheme, the exact Hopf ideal, and the minimality proof. No equality with the candidate is claimed.

The next stage is UF-041: derive the quotient law and test whether the quotient morphism extends across the three nilpotent ambient parameters.
