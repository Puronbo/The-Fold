# The observer's thermometer: acceleration, the Unruh period, and the same 2π

## The claim

The clock note gave the horizon a temperature through its **Killing** clock: `κ = c⁴/(4GM)` and
`T = ħκ/(2πck_B)`. The observer note gave the probe two parameters, `(d, w)`. This note joins them. An
accelerated observer carries a thermometer: the Minkowski vacuum looks thermal, with

```
T = ħ a/(2π c k_B)
```

where `a` is the proper acceleration. The `2π` is the same `2π` as the horizon clock's Euclidean period,
and the null surface is the same null crease. But the Fold's `k` is a **homothety**, not a boost, so the
Fold does not derive the Unruh effect. The exact content is the Rindler geometry, the detector's detailed
balance, and the near-horizon equivalence-principle bridge — separated from the rhyme.

## The Rindler wedge

An observer with constant proper acceleration `a` moves on a hyperbola. In the observer's Rindler frame,
with `ρ` the proper distance to the horizon,

```
ds² = −(aρ/c)² dt² + dρ² + dy² + dz²
```

The observer sits at `ρ = c²/a`, where `g_tt = −c²` and `t` is proper time. The horizon is `ρ = 0`: the
Killing vector `∂_t` is null there, so the surface is generated by a null Killing vector, exactly as the
Schwarzschild horizon is. The proper acceleration of a static observer at radius `ρ` is `c²/ρ`; at the
observer's radius it is `a`.

The Euclidean continuation `t → −iτ` gives

```
ds²_E = (aρ/c)² dτ² + dρ² + dy² + dz²
```

Regularity at `ρ = 0` requires the circumference `(aρ/c) τ_period` to equal `2πρ`, so

```
τ_period = 2π c/a,        T = ħ/(k_B τ_period) = ħ a/(2π c k_B)
```

This is the Unruh period. The `2π` is the circle's, not a new constant.

| acceleration | `T` (K) | period `2πc/a` | Rindler distance `c²/a` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1 g` = 9.807 m/s² | 3.977e−20 | 1.921e8 s (6.087 yr) | 9.165e15 m (0.969 ly) |
| `10 g` | 3.977e−19 | 1.921e7 s | 9.165e14 m |
| `100 g` | 3.977e−18 | 1.921e6 s | 9.165e13 m |
| Sun's horizon `κ` = 1.522e13 m/s² | 6.170e−8 | 1.238e−4 s | 5.907e3 m = `2r_s` |

The last row is the horizon's surface gravity, not the Sun's surface gravity. The two are different
numbers: the Sun's surface acceleration is `274.2 m/s²`, giving `T = 1.112e−18 K`, while its horizon's
`κ` gives the Hawking temperature `6.170e−8 K`. The factor between them is `(r_s/R)²/√f ≈ 1.8e−11`.

At one `g` the temperature is `3.98e−20 K`, because one `g` is about `5.7e50` times smaller than the
Planck acceleration (`5.561e51 m/s²`); the ratio of temperatures is `2.81e−52`. The period is `6.09 yr`,
and the Rindler horizon is about `0.97 ly` away. The Unruh effect is not small because the formula is
weak; it is small because everyday acceleration is tiny.

## The detector: detailed balance

The Unruh–DeWitt detector is a two-level system coupled to the field along the trajectory. In the
Minkowski vacuum, the transition rates obey

```
P(excitation)/P(de-excitation) = e^{−E/(k_B T)}
```

with `T = ħ a/(2π c k_B)`. For `E = k_B T`, the ratio is

```
e^{−1} = 0.367879
```

so de-excitation is about `2.72` times as likely as excitation. The Planck factor is the signature of the
thermal state, and it follows from the KMS condition: the correlation function is periodic in imaginary
time with period `ħ/(k_B T) = 2πc/a`. The detector's response is the Fourier transform of the Wightman
function along the hyperbola; the periodicity is what makes the spectrum Planckian.

| `E/(k_B T)` | ratio `e^{−E/(k_B T)}` |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.606531 |
| 1.0 | 0.367879 |
| 2.0 | 0.135335 |
| 5.0 | 6.7379e−3 |

This is exact for the uniformly accelerated detector in the Minkowski vacuum. It is a reading of the
period, not a derivation of the period from the Fold.

## The equivalence principle: two temperatures

The equivalence principle makes the local geometry of a static observer in a gravitational field
indistinguishable from an accelerated observer in flat space. The local statement is the Unruh
temperature of the local vacuum; a detector in the global Hartle–Hawking state sees the Tolman-redshifted
Hawking temperature instead. For Schwarzschild,

```
ds² = −f c² dt² + f^{−1} dr² + r² dΩ²,   f = 1 − r_s/r
```

a static observer at radius `r` has proper acceleration

```
a = GM/(r²√f) = κ (r_s/r)²/√f,        κ = c⁴/(4GM) = GM/r_s²
```

so the local Unruh temperature is

```
T_U = ħ a/(2π c k_B) = T_H (r_s/r)²/√f,        T_H = ħκ/(2π c k_B)
```

The Hawking temperature at infinity is `T_H`. The **Tolman** relation for a thermal bath in equilibrium is

```
T_local √f = T_∞
```

which gives `T_local = T_H/√f`. That is the redshift of the Hawking radiation, not the local Unruh
temperature of a static detector. The two agree near the horizon, where `a ≈ κ/√f` and `(r_s/r)² ≈ 1`;
away from it, they differ.

| radius | `√f` | `T_U/T_H` | Tolman `1/√f` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1.01 r_s` | 0.09950 | 9.852 | 10.05 |
| `2 r_s` | 0.70711 | 0.3536 | 1.414 |
| `10 r_s` | 0.94868 | 0.01054 | 1.054 |
| `100 r_s` | 0.99499 | 1.01e−4 | 1.005 |

The exact identity is the product, not the ratio:

```
T_U (r/r_s)² √f = T_H
```

At the horizon both temperatures diverge, but the product stays finite. The near-horizon limit is the
bridge: `a√f/κ → 1` as `r → r_s`; at `1.01 r_s` the ratio is `0.9803`, and at `2 r_s` it is `0.25`. The
equivalence principle is local, and the two global states — Minkowski vacuum versus Hartle–Hawking — are
what distinguish the two temperatures.

This is the correction to the naive statement "`T_local√f = T_∞` is the Unruh temperature." It is the
Tolman redshift of the Hawking bath. The local Unruh temperature is `ħ a/(2π)`, and the two coincide only
where the observer is close enough to the horizon that the local frame is the Rindler frame.

## The Fold's `k` is a homothety, not a boost

The Fold's scale is `r → kr`, or `g → k²g`. That is a **homothety**: a conformal transformation with a
nonzero divergence, not an isometry. A Lorentz boost is an isometry, `g → g`, with zero divergence. The
distinction is exact:

| object | transformation | generator | divergence | invariant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fold scale | `x → kx` | `x^μ ∂_μ` | `n` | ratios |
| Lorentz boost | `x → Λx` | `x ∂_t + t ∂_x` | `0` | `t² − x²` |
| Rindler boost | orbit of the boost | Killing | `0` | the wedge |

The Rindler horizon is the set where the boost Killing vector becomes null; the Unruh effect requires the
boost invariance of the Minkowski vacuum. The Fold's `k` has no such vector. It is dimensionless,
while an acceleration has units of `m/s²`; there is no map from `k` to `a` without a length. The Fold's
`T ∝ 1/k` is the horizon clock's exponent, not the Unruh law `T ∝ a`. The two laws have different
variables: `k` is a scale, `a` is an acceleration.

The shared objects are real:

- the **null crease**: the Rindler horizon is the flat-space case of the Fold horizon's null crease;
- the **`2π` period**: the Euclidean circle is the full turn;
- the **observer's window**: the Rindler wedge is the causal window.

The Fold's horizon gets its temperature from the surface gravity of its Schwarzschild reading, not from
an acceleration. That is why the clock note could close `T ∝ 1/k` without a boost, and why this note
cannot close `T ∝ a` from `k`.

## The same `2π`

| where | period | reason |
|---|---|---|
| Rindler | `2πc/a` | Euclidean regularity at `ρ = 0` |
| Schwarzschild | `2πc/κ` | Euclidean regularity at `r = r_s` |
| Fold closure | `2π/m` per sector, `2π` full turn | the `m`-fold divides the full turn |
| sampling | `πR/m` half-sector, Nyquist `N > 2m` | discrete closure |

The `2π` is the same number, but the reason differs: regularity of the Euclidean cone versus closure of
a full turn. The Fold's `2π` belongs to the turn, not to the Unruh period. Do not claim the Fold derives
the Unruh period from the closure; the resemblance is the circle, not the physics.

## What this does and does not say

- It **does** give the exact Rindler geometry: `ds² = −(aρ/c)²dt² + dρ² + …`, horizon at `ρ = 0`,
  proper acceleration `c²/ρ`.
- It **does** derive the Unruh period `2πc/a` and the temperature `T = ħa/(2πck_B)` from Euclidean
  regularity, for the uniformly accelerated observer in the Minkowski vacuum.
- It **does** give the detector's detailed balance `e^{−E/(k_B T)}` and the KMS period.
- It **does** separate the local Unruh temperature `T_U = T_H(r_s/r)²/√f` from the Tolman-redshifted
  Hawking temperature `T_H/√f`; they agree only near the horizon.
- It **does** show the Fold's `k` is a homothety, not a boost: it is dimensionless and conformal, with
  no Killing vector and no acceleration.
- It **does** identify the shared objects — the null crease, the `2π`, the causal window — as rhymes,
  not derivations.
- It **does not** derive the Unruh effect, the KMS condition, or the Einstein equation from the Fold.
- It **does not** claim the Fold's `k` is an acceleration or that `T ∝ 1/k` is the Unruh law.
- It **does not** claim the Fold's `2π` is the Euclidean period; it is the full turn of the twist.
- It **does not** treat rotating or charged horizons, or the trans-Planckian issue.
- It **does not** claim the local temperature is global; it is the response of a local detector.

## The one sentence

**An accelerated observer sees a thermal bath at `T = ħa/(2πck_B)`, set by the same `2π` and the same
null crease as the horizon clock; the Fold's `k` is a homothety, not a boost, so the shared object is the
null geometry and the period, not a derivation.**
