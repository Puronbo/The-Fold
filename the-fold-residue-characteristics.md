# UF-021 — Complete residue-characteristic classification of the rank-eight family

## The family and its monic presentation

Let k be an algebraically closed field and
\[
R_{p,q}=k[x,y]/(x²+y²-p,\ x²y²-q).
\]
Eliminate y² using y²=p−x². The second relation becomes
\[
x²(p−x²)=q,\qquad f(T)=T⁴-pT²+q=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
R_{p,q}\cong k[x,y]/(f(x),\ y²-(p-x²)).
\]
Both defining polynomials are monic in the successive variables. Thus
\[
1,x,x²,x³,\ y,xy,x²y,x³y
\]
is a k-basis (up to ordering), and every fiber has length eight in every characteristic. No residue characteristic changes the rank.

## Theorem 1 — odd-characteristic fibers

Assume char(k) is odd.

1. The fiber is étale if and only if
\[
q(p²-4q)\ne0.
\]
2. If q=0 and p≠0, it consists of four geometric points, each of local length two. The multiplication operators Mx and My have Jordan block sizes [2,2,1,1,1,1].
3. If p²−4q=0 and p≠0, it consists of four geometric points, each of local length two. Both Mx and My have Jordan block sizes [2,2,2,2].
4. At p=q=0, it is the single local algebra k[x,y]/(y²+x²,x⁴), with Hilbert function (1,2,2,2,1), Loewy length five, and one-dimensional socle generated by x³y.

**Proof sketch.** The quotient map is (x,y)↦(p,q)=(x²+y²,x²y²), whose Jacobian is J=4xy(x²−y²). Since 4 is invertible, J vanishes exactly on the four reflection lines x=0, y=0, x=y, x=−y. Their images are q=0 and p²−4q=0. Off these images the finite fiber is unramified, hence étale. On q=0,p≠0, the four points are (0,±√p),(±√p,0); the local transverse equation has a double zero, giving length two. On p²−4q=0,p≠0, x²=y²=p/2 and the four independent sign choices give four points; each is a simple reflection ramification, again length two. The Jordan types are the characteristic-zero calculations: the relevant local double directions remain nonzero because 2 and 4 are invertible. At the vertex, the displayed local presentation and monomial reduction give the Hilbert function and socle exactly as in UF-019. ∎

The trace discriminant specializes to
\[
\det(\operatorname{Tr}(b_i b_j))=2^{16}q^4(p²-4q)^4.
\]
For odd characteristic its scalar factor is a unit, so it detects exactly the two branch components. Thus no odd residue characteristic creates an additional discriminant component or changes the listed geometric fiber strata.

## Theorem 2 — characteristic two is globally inseparable

Assume char(k)=2. The partial derivatives of both defining equations vanish identically:
\[
\partial_x(x²+y²-p)=\partial_y(x²+y²-p)=0,
\]
\[
\partial_x(x²y²-q)=\partial_y(x²y²-q)=0.
\]
Equivalently, the quotient Jacobian J=4xy(x²−y²) is identically zero. Since each fiber is still finite of length eight by the monic presentation, none is étale: every geometric point is ramified. The trace-pairing determinant formula also reduces to zero for every (p,q).

This does **not** mean the abstract vertex algebra loses its Frobenius property. UF-020's coefficient pairing still has determinant 1 in characteristic two. The distinction is:
- the fiber algebra as an eight-dimensional vector space remains free of the same rank;
- the vertex's perfect Frobenius pairing survives;
- the parameter map is everywhere inseparable and its trace pairing is degenerate in every characteristic-two fiber.

## Arithmetic conclusion

For the parameterized rank-eight family, characteristic two is the unique exceptional residue characteristic at the level of generic étaleness and trace discriminant. Every odd characteristic has the same two-branch discriminant geometry and the same three fiber strata, with the parameter values interpreted in that field.

This is a precise and limited “bad prime” statement. It does not assert that every arithmetic specialization over number fields has identical rational-point behavior: splitting fields and the number of k-rational points can vary with p and q. The theorem concerns geometric fibers and their scheme-theoretic local structure.
