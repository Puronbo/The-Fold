# UF-050 — The second-Frobenius candidate retains the full nilpotent thickening

## 1. Coefficient decomposition

In characteristic two, the full automorphism scheme has coordinate algebra
\[
A=k[c,d_0,h,\mathbf t,\Delta^{-1}]/(c^4,d_0^2,h^2),
\qquad \Delta=ai-bh,
\]
where \(\mathbf t\) denotes the remaining substitution coefficients.

Put
\[
T=k[c,d_0,h]/(c^4,d_0^2,h^2).
\]
Since \(h^2=0\), the element \(bh\) is nilpotent. The relation \(\Delta=ai-bh\) and invertibility of \(\Delta\) imply that \(ai=\Delta+bh\) is invertible. Conversely, if \(ai\) is invertible, then \(\Delta=ai(1-(ai)^{-1}bh)\) is invertible because the second factor is one minus a nilpotent. Hence localizing at \(\Delta\) is equivalent to localizing at \(ai\), and the latter uses only the reduced coefficient variables.

Consequently there is an algebra decomposition
\[
\boxed{A\cong A_{\mathrm{red}}\otimes_k T}
\]
where the tensor notation is understood with the reduced coordinate algebra carrying the remaining variables and determinant localization. In particular, A is free of rank 16 over A_red.

## 2. The candidate ideal comes from the reduced quotient

The second-Frobenius candidate \(\mathscr K_2\) is the kernel of the composite
\[
G\longrightarrow G_{\mathrm{red}}\longrightarrow Q
\xrightarrow{F_{Q/k}^{(2)}} Q^{(2)}.
\]
Its defining ideal is generated by the pullbacks of the second-Frobenius kernel equations on Q. In the global coordinates of UF-046/047 these are
\[
a^4-1,\quad b^4,\quad i^4-1,\quad
\frac{\phi^4}{i^4},\quad
\frac{\alpha^4}{a^4}
+\frac{b^4\phi^4}{a^4i^4}
+\frac{\eta^4}{i^4}.
\]
None of these equations involves c,d₀,h. They are pulled back from A_red.

Therefore, if \(J_2\subseteq A_{\mathrm{red}}\) denotes the reduced-coordinate ideal of the Frobenius-kernel preimage, then
\[
A/I(\mathscr K_2)
\cong (A_{\mathrm{red}}/J_2)\otimes_k T.
\]
This identity is the precise algebraic meaning of the statement that the candidate retains the full ambient nilpotent thickening.

## 3. Theorem UF-050.1 — Associated graded of the candidate

The monomials
\[
c^r d_0^s h^t,
\qquad 0\le r\le3,\quad 0\le s,t\le1,
\]
form a basis of T. With the ordinary total-degree filtration from the ideal \(\mathfrak n=(c,d_0,h)\),
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak n}T;u)
=(1+u+u^2+u^3)(1+u)^2
=1+3u+4u^2+4u^3+3u^4+u^5.
\]
Because the candidate ideal is extended from the reduced coordinate algebra, tensoring the associated-graded calculation gives
\[
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak n}
\bigl(A/I(\mathscr K_2)\bigr)
\cong
(A_{\mathrm{red}}/J_2)\otimes_k
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak n}T.
\]
Thus its graded ranks over the reduced candidate are
\[
\boxed{1,3,4,4,3,1}
\]
in degrees zero through five, and the positive-degree ranks are \(3,4,4,3,1\).

In particular, the degree-two piece has rank four:
\[
\mathfrak n^2/\mathfrak n^3
\cong (A_{\mathrm{red}}/J_2)^{\oplus4}
\]
as a module over the reduced candidate coordinate algebra.

## 4. What this says—and does not say—about the normal closure

We know \(\mathscr K\subseteq\mathscr K_2\), so
\[
I(\mathscr K_2)\subseteq I(\mathscr K).
\]
The calculation above determines the associated-graded structure of the larger candidate quotient. It does not determine whether the smaller normal-closure ideal imposes additional higher-order relations.

The tangent-space equality and the inclusion constrain the first-order terms, but do not force equality in degrees two through five. The degree-two comparison remains the first unresolved test.
