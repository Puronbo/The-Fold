# UF-018 — Local algebras and Jordan structure of the Fold fibers

## 1. Family and strata

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
\[
R_{p,q}=k[x,y]/(x^2+y^2-p,\ x^2y^2-q),
\]
a complete intersection of length eight. The quotient base in the original z-coordinate has q=(p²-z²)/2.

The discriminant has reduced components q=0 and p²-4q=0. Their intersection in the (p,q)-plane is only (p,q)=(0,0).

## 2. Generic point of q=0

Take p≠0 and q=0. The fiber equations are
\[
x^2+y^2=p,\qquad x^2y^2=0.
\]
There are four geometric points:
\[
(0,\sqrt p),\ (0,-\sqrt p),\ (\sqrt p,0),\ (-\sqrt p,0).
\]
At each point one coordinate is a unit and the other has square zero. Each local algebra is isomorphic to k[ε]/(ε²), hence has length two. Total length is 4·2=8.

For a representative p=1,q=0, the Jordan form of Mx has blocks
\[
J_2(0),J_2(0),[-1],[-1],[1],[1].
\]
The same block-size pattern holds for My. The trace-pairing rank is four.

## 3. Generic point of p²-4q=0

Take p≠0 and q=p²/4. Then
\[
(x^2-y^2)^2=p^2-4q=0.
\]
The four geometric points are
\[
x=\pm\sqrt{p/2},\qquad y=\pm\sqrt{p/2},
\]
with independent signs. At each point the transverse equation has a double zero, so each local algebra has length two, isomorphic to k[ε]/(ε²). Again the total length is eight.

For p=2,q=1, Mx has two size-two Jordan blocks at eigenvalue +1 and two size-two blocks at eigenvalue −1. My has the same Jordan block-size pattern. The trace-pairing rank is four.

## 4. Intersection / vertex

At p=q=0,
\[
R_{0,0}=k[x,y]/(x^2+y^2,\ x^2y^2).
\]
Using y²=−x², the second relation becomes x⁴=0. Thus
\[
R_{0,0}\cong k[x,y]/(y^2+x^2,x^4)
\]
has basis (1,x,x²,x³,y,xy,x²y,x³y), length eight, and only one geometric point.

Both Mx and My have Jordan type
\[
\boxed{[4,4]}
\]
(two nilpotent Jordan blocks of size four). The trace pairing has rank one: only the unit direction contributes a nonzero trace at this fiber. The maximal ideal is generated by x,y; its powers and the associated graded algebra provide further local information.

## 5. Theorem UF-018.1 — Fiber stratification

The length-eight fibers have the following strata:

| Stratum | Geometric points | Local lengths | Trace rank | Jordan type of Mx |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| q=0, p≠0 | 4 | 2 each | 4 | [2,2,1,1,1,1] |
| p²−4q=0, p≠0 | 4 | 2 each | 4 | [2,2,2,2] |
| p=q=0 | 1 | 8 | 1 | [4,4] |

The same Jordan block-size patterns hold for My in these representative fibers.

**Proof.** The point sets follow by solving the two fiber equations. At a generic point of either branch, the local transverse equation is a square-zero parameter, giving dual numbers and length two. At the intersection, the displayed complete-intersection presentation has length 2·4=8 and support only at the origin. Specializing the multiplication matrices gives the stated Jordan forms. The trace ranks follow by specializing the Gram matrix and computing its rank.

## 6. What this proves—and what it does not

The two branch components have the same number of geometric points, the same local lengths, and the same trace rank. Their individual multiplication-operator Jordan forms differ, so the operator data distinguishes them. At the vertex, the fiber is concentrated at one point and has a much larger nilpotent structure.

A single operator's Jordan form is not a complete invariant of a finite commutative algebra: the pair of commuting operators together with the unit vector and their relations is the faithful algebra representation. The local algebra or the full pair is needed for classification.

These are fiberwise statements over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. They do not establish a prime-number theorem or an eigenprime correspondence.
