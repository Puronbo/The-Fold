# UF-041 — The second-Frobenius quotient map and a canonical thickening

Work over a perfect field k of characteristic two (in particular, over the algebraically closed field used in UF-033–UF-040). Let \(\mathscr A=\operatorname{Aut}(k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2))\), let \(\mathscr A_{\rm red}\) be its reduced subgroup, and let \(N\triangleleft\mathscr A_{\rm red}\) be the eight-dimensional reduced normal closure found in UF-039.

Set
\[
Q=\mathscr A_{\rm red}/N,\qquad \dim Q=5.
\]

## 1. Why the first Frobenius is not uniformly sufficient

The ambient automorphism scheme has coordinate parameters \(c,d,h\) satisfying
\[
c^4=d^2=h^2=0.
\]
The nilpotent coordinate c can have nonzero square: the relation \(c^4=0\) does not imply \(c^2=0\). Thus the first relative Frobenius does not, on the basis of the ambient equations alone, annihilate the full nilradical.

The second relative Frobenius does:
\[
c^4=d^4=h^4=0.
\]
Because the nilradical is generated by c,d,h, its fourth-power image is zero in characteristic two. This is the uniform Frobenius exponent guaranteed by the presentation in UF-033.

## 2. Theorem UF-041.1 — A canonical morphism to the twice-twisted reduced quotient

Let
\[
F_{\mathscr A}^{(2)}:\mathscr A\longrightarrow\mathscr A^{(2)}
\]
be the second relative Frobenius. On coordinate rings it sends a function to its fourth power (with the usual Frobenius twist on scalars).

The pullback of every nilpotent generator of the defining ideal of \(\mathscr A_{\rm red}^{(2)}\) is zero, since
\[
c^4=d^4=h^4=0.
\]
Therefore the morphism factors through the closed reduced subgroup twist:
\[
\mathscr A\longrightarrow\mathscr A_{\rm red}^{(2)}.
\]
Composing with the Frobenius twist of the quotient morphism gives a canonical homomorphism of group schemes
\[
\boxed{
\Psi_2:\mathscr A
\xrightarrow{F_{\mathscr A}^{(2)}}
\mathscr A_{\rm red}^{(2)}
\longrightarrow Q^{(2)}.
}
\]
This avoids the generally invalid assumption that there is a projection from a non-reduced scheme to its reduction.

## 3. Theorem UF-041.2 — The kernel is a normal thickening with the required support and tangent space

Define
\[
\mathscr K_2:=\ker(\Psi_2).
\]
Then \(\mathscr K_2\) is a closed normal subgroup scheme of \(\mathscr A\).

Its reduced support is N. Indeed, on geometric points over a perfect algebraically closed field, relative Frobenius is bijective on points; the reduced quotient map has kernel N. Hence
\[
\boxed{(\mathscr K_2)_{\rm red}=N,\qquad \dim(\mathscr K_2)_{\rm red}=8.}
\]

The differential of the second relative Frobenius is zero in characteristic two. Thus the differential of \(\Psi_2\) is zero and
\[
\boxed{T_1\mathscr K_2=T_1\mathscr A,\qquad
\dim T_1\mathscr K_2=16.}
\]
Consequently \(\mathscr K_2\) has exactly the support and tangent-space dimensions forced for the scheme-theoretic normal closure in UF-039.

Since U lies in N on reduced points and \(\Psi_2\) factors through the reduced quotient after Frobenius, U is contained scheme-theoretically in \(\mathscr K_2\). Therefore the scheme-theoretic normal closure \(\mathscr K\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{\mathscr K\subseteq\mathscr K_2.}
\]

This is a substantive upper bound: it constructs a concrete normal subgroup scheme containing U, rather than merely predicting one from tangent dimensions.

## 4. Local size of the candidate

The smooth five-dimensional group Q has a first Frobenius kernel of height one and a second Frobenius kernel of height two. The kernel of its second relative Frobenius is supported at the identity; in regular local parameters \(t_1,\ldots,t_5\), its local ideal is generated by
\[
(t_1^4,t_2^4,t_3^4,t_4^4,t_5^4).
\]
Its length over the algebraically closed field is therefore
\[
4^5=1024.
\]
This is the quotient-side Frobenius kernel. It does not by itself give the length of \(\mathscr K_2\), whose fibers over N and global group structure must be accounted for.

## 5. What remains unresolved

The construction does **not** prove \(\mathscr K=\mathscr K_2\). It proves only
\[
\mathscr K\subseteq\mathscr K_2.
\]
Both groups have the same reduced support N and the same tangent space at the identity, but they may differ in higher nilpotent order.

The exact next task is to determine whether conjugates of U generate all of \(\mathscr K_2\), or whether their Hopf ideal defines a strictly smaller intermediate thickening. This requires scheme-valued conjugation over test algebras with nilpotents of orders up to four, not just dual numbers.

## 6. Research status

**Proved:** the second relative Frobenius gives a canonical morphism from the full automorphism scheme to \(Q^{(2)}\); its kernel \(\mathscr K_2\) is normal, contains U, has reduced support N, and has tangent dimension 16.

**Open:** whether \(\mathscr K\) equals \(\mathscr K_2\), and the exact Hopf ideal of the minimal normal closure.

The first-Frobenius candidate in UF-040 should therefore be treated as superseded by this second-Frobenius construction unless a separate first-Frobenius factorization is explicitly established.
