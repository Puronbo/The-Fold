# UF-025 — The graded automorphism group and enlargement beyond D4

## Homogeneous presentation

Over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero,
\[
C=k[x,y]/(x²+y²,x²y²)=k[x,y]/(q,x⁴),
\qquad q=x²+y²,
\]
because modulo q we have x²y²=−x⁴. This is a standard graded algebra with Hilbert function (1,2,2,2,1).

A graded automorphism is determined by an invertible linear change of degree-one generators. We classify this subgroup of Aut_alg(C); we do not claim here to classify all possibly nonlinear algebra automorphisms.

## Theorem 1 — Classification of graded linear automorphisms

Let
\[
A=tQ,\qquad t\in k^*,\quad Q\in O(2,k),
\]
where Q has first row (a,b). Then A induces a graded automorphism of C if and only if
\[
ab(a²-b²)=0.
\]
Consequently the orthogonal part consists of 16 matrices: eight rotations and eight reflections. It is the dihedral group of order 16, generated by a rotation through 45 degrees and a reflection. The full graded automorphism group is the central product
\[
(k^*\times D_{16})/\langle(-1,-I)\rangle,
\]
where D16 denotes the group of order 16.

**Proof.** The degree-two part of the defining ideal is the line kq, so any graded automorphism must satisfy q(A(x,y))=λq for λ≠0. Hence A=tQ with Q orthogonal after absorbing a square root of λ into t (available because k is algebraically closed).

Write the first row of Q as (a,b), so a²+b²=1. The image of x is t(ax+by). In C,
\[
(ax+by)^4
=4t^4ab(a²-b²)x³y,
\]
because x⁴=y⁴=x²y²=0 and xy³=−x³y. Since x³y spans the nonzero degree-four component, preservation of x⁴=0 is equivalent to ab(a²−b²)=0. For an orthogonal matrix the second row is ±(−b,a), and the same condition guarantees the corresponding inverse condition. The allowed first rows are exactly
\[
(±1,0),\ (0,±1),\
(±1/\sqrt2,±1/\sqrt2),\
(±1/\sqrt2,\mp1/\sqrt2).
\]
There are eight; either orientation gives two orthogonal matrices for each row, hence 16. They form the dihedral symmetry group of the eight equally spaced directions, D16. Scalars t give the central k* factor, with the common element −I identified. ∎

## Corollary — D4 is a proper subgroup

The original signed-permutation D4 is the subgroup generated by a 90-degree rotation and a coordinate reflection. It has order eight and index two in the orthogonal D16 subgroup. The additional 45-degree rotation is a genuine graded algebra automorphism.

An explicit unnormalized representative is
\[
A_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad x\mapsto x-y,\quad y\mapsto x+y.
\]
It satisfies
\[
q\mapsto 2q,\qquad (x-y)^4=(x+y)^4=0\quad\text{in }C.
\]
After dividing by √2 it is an order-eight rotation. It is not a signed permutation, so the original D4 action is not the full graded automorphism group.

## What this does and does not establish

- The D4 symmetry is intrinsic as a subgroup of algebra automorphisms, but it is not the full graded symmetry group.
- The graded algebra has a larger orthogonal symmetry D16, up to scalar dilations.
- This result does not classify nonlinear automorphisms that may add higher-degree terms to x and y.
- It does not show that D4 is uniquely recoverable from the abstract algebra alone. A further intrinsic characterization would need extra data, such as a selected pair of degree-one axes or a chosen quotient map.
