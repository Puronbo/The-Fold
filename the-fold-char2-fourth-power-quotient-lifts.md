# UF-046/047 — Global fourth-power lifts of the reduced quotient coordinates

## Setup

Let k have characteristic two and
\[
C=k[x,z]/(x^4,z^2).
\]
Write a full automorphism as
\[
X=c+ax+bz+\alpha x^2+\cdots,\qquad
Z=d_0+hx+iz+\phi x^2+\eta xz+\cdots.
\]
The automorphism scheme is defined by
\[
c^4=0,\qquad d_0^2=0,\qquad h^2=0,
\]
with
\[
\Delta=ai-bh\in A^\times.
\]

## Reduced quotient coordinates

On the reduction, c=d_0=h=0, and the graded coefficients a,i are units. Normalizing by the graded substitution gives the quotient coordinates
\[
F=\frac{\phi}{i},\qquad
R=\frac{\alpha}{a}+\frac{b\phi}{ai}+\frac{\eta}{i}.
\]
These are the two additive coordinates in
\[
Q=\mathbb G_a^2\rtimes B,
\]
where B is the graded subgroup.

## Theorem UF-046 — Fourth powers lift uniquely

The nilradical of A is generated by c,d_0,h. Every element n of this ideal satisfies n^4=0: fourth powers are additive in characteristic two, and c^4=d_0^4=h^4=0.

Consequently, if two elements of A lift the same element of A_red, their fourth powers agree. Thus every fourth power in A_red has a unique lift to A.

In particular, the pullbacks of F^4 and R^4 are
\[
\widetilde F=\frac{\phi^4}{i^4},\qquad
\widetilde R=
\frac{\alpha^4}{a^4}
+\frac{b^4\phi^4}{a^4i^4}
+\frac{\eta^4}{i^4}.
\]

## Theorem UF-047 — The formulas are global

Although the displayed formulas were first derived on the localization where a and i are invertible, that localization is the entire automorphism scheme.

Indeed, h^2=0 implies (bh)^2=0, so bh is nilpotent. Since Delta=ai-bh is a unit and the characteristic is two,
\[
ai=\Delta+bh.
\]
A unit plus a nilpotent element is a unit. Hence ai is a unit. In a commutative ring, if a product is a unit then each factor is a unit. Therefore
\[
a,i\in A^\times.
\]
The formulas for F, R, and their fourth powers are consequently globally regular on the full determinant-open automorphism scheme. There is no second chart and no overlap-gluing problem.

## Frobenius-kernel candidate

The second relative Frobenius quotient equations are globally
\[
a^4-1=0,\quad b^4=0,\quad i^4-1=0,\quad
\widetilde F=0,\quad \widetilde R=0.
\]
They define the kernel of the composite from the full automorphism group scheme through its reduction and the quotient Q, followed by the second relative Frobenius of Q. This is a normal subgroup scheme containing U and having reduced support N.

## What this does not prove

Globality and normality do not imply minimality. The scheme-theoretic normal closure K of U is contained in this Frobenius kernel K_2, but it remains necessary to prove whether K=K_2. Matching reduced support and tangent dimension is not enough to identify higher infinitesimal structure.
