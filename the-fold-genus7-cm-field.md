# G-29 — integral basis and finite-prime behavior of the CM extension

Let
\[
g(Y)=Y^4+4Y^3-2Y^2-20Y-14,\qquad K=\mathbb Q(\beta),\quad g(\beta)=0.
\]

## 1. Full ring of integers

The polynomial is Eisenstein at 2. Hence over \(\mathbb Q_2\) it defines a totally ramified degree-four extension, and \(\beta\) is a uniformizer. For a totally ramified extension generated by a uniformizer, the local integer ring is generated by that uniformizer. Therefore
\[
\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_2=\mathbb Z_2[\beta].
\]
The polynomial discriminant is \(2^8\cdot17\). The square of the global index \([\mathcal O_K:\mathbb Z[\beta]]\) divides this discriminant. Its 2-part is zero by the local uniformizer argument; its 17-part is zero because the discriminant has 17-adic valuation one. No other prime can divide the index. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\beta],\qquad
\operatorname{disc}(K)=2^8\cdot17.}
\]

The unique prime \(\mathfrak p\) above 2 has residue degree one and ramification index four. In particular
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta)=1,\qquad v_{\mathfrak p}(2)=4.
\]

## 2. Relative quadratic extension

Let \(L=\mathbb Q(\pi)\), with \(\beta=\pi+2/\pi\). Then
\[
L=K(\pi),\qquad \pi^2-\beta\pi+2=0.
\]
The discriminant of this quadratic order generator is
\[
\delta=\beta^2-8.
\]
The exact resultant/norm computation gives
\[
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\delta)=\operatorname{Res}(g,Y^2-8)=4.
\]
Thus the principal ideal \((\delta)\) is supported only at the unique prime \(\mathfrak p\) over 2, and its valuation is exactly two:
\[
(\delta)=\mathfrak p^2.
\]

At \(\mathfrak p\), the Newton polygon of \(X^2-\beta X+2\) has coefficient points
\[
(0,4),\ (1,1),\ (2,0),
\]
and slopes \(-3\) and \(-1\), each of horizontal length one. The local Newton-polygon factorization theorem groups roots of distinct valuations into factors over the complete base field. Here both factors have degree one, so
\[
L\otimes_K K_{\mathfrak p}\cong K_{\mathfrak p}\times K_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
In particular the quadratic algebra is split, hence has zero relative discriminant exponent at \(\mathfrak p\).

At every other finite prime, \(\delta\) is a unit, so the order \(\mathcal O_K[\pi]\) already has unit discriminant there and the extension is unramified. Combining the finite places gives
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak d_{L/K}=\mathcal O_K.}
\]
Thus this CM quadratic extension is unramified at every finite prime, while its four real places become complex.

## 3. Certificate and scope

The standard-library script certificates/g29_cm_extension_arithmetic.py checks the exact polynomial discriminant, Eisenstein divisibility, norm resultant, and local Newton polygon. The proof that the quartic order is maximal uses the local uniformizer theorem and the discriminant-index divisibility argument. The relative unramifiedness conclusion uses the local Newton-polygon factorization theorem.

This does not yet identify the class of L/K in the narrow class group or determine whether it is the unique quadratic extension unramified at all finite primes.

## 4. Ordinary class number

The Minkowski bound for the totally real quartic is
\[
\frac{4!}{4^4}\sqrt{4352}=\frac32\sqrt{17}<7.
\]
Every ideal class has an integral representative of norm at most six. The defining polynomial is irreducible modulo 3 and modulo 5, so both primes are inert and there are no prime ideals of norms 3 or 5. The unique prime \(\mathfrak p=(2,\beta)\) above 2 is principal because
\[
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta+2)=g(-2)=2,
\]
so \((\beta+2)=\mathfrak p\). Every ideal of norm at most six is therefore principal. Hence
\[
\boxed{h_K=1.}
\]
This is the ordinary class number. Section 5 below proves the narrow class number is exactly two and computes the full unit-signature image. The finite checks and norm computation are in certificates/g30_real_quartic_class_number.py.


## 5. Narrow class number and class-field identification

The following three algebraic integers are units, since their exact norms are one:
\[
\epsilon_1=1+\beta,\qquad
\epsilon_2=1+3\beta+\beta^2,\qquad
\epsilon_3=-1-4\beta-4\beta^2-\beta^3.
\]
The four real roots of g are isolated in the rational intervals
\[
(-2.81,-2.80),\quad(-2.53,-2.52),\quad
(-0.89,-0.88),\quad(2.22,2.23).
\]
Exact rational interval evaluation gives their signature vectors, in this ordering:
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\epsilon_1)=(-,-,+,+),\quad
\operatorname{sgn}(\epsilon_2)=(+,-,-,+),\quad
\operatorname{sgn}(\epsilon_3)=(+,-,+,-).
\]
These three vectors are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_2\), and each has even parity. Thus the unit-signature image contains the full order-eight even-parity subgroup.

For a totally real quartic, the narrow class number is
\[
h_K^+=h_K\,\frac{16}{|\operatorname{sgn}(\mathcal O_K^\times)|}.
\]
Since \(h_K=1\), the signature image has size at least eight, so \(h_K^+\le2\). But the already established nontrivial quadratic extension \(L/K\) is unramified at every finite prime; by narrow class field theory it is contained in the narrow Hilbert class field. Hence \(h_K^+\ge2\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{h_K^+=2,\qquad
\operatorname{sgn}(\mathcal O_K^\times)=\{(\pm1)^4:
\text{even number of minus signs}\}.}
\]
The narrow Hilbert class field has degree two over K. Since L/K is itself quadratic and unramified at every finite prime, it is exactly that field:
\[
\boxed{L=K^{\mathrm{narrow\ Hilbert}}.}
\]
The exact unit norms, rational root intervals, interval sign checks, and rank-three signature calculation are included in certificates/g30_real_quartic_class_number.py.


## 6. Local splitting recovers the fourfold Newton slopes

The two degree-one Newton-polygon factors of \(X^2-\beta X+2\) over \(K_{\mathfrak p}\) have root valuations 1 and 3, respectively, for the normalization \(v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta)=1\). Since \(v_{\mathfrak p}(2)=4\), the corresponding normalized 2-adic valuations are
\[
\frac{1}{4},\qquad\frac{3}{4}.
\]
The decomposition
\[
L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_2
\cong K_{\mathfrak p}\times K_{\mathfrak p}
\]
has two factors of degree four over \(\mathbb Q_2\). In each factor the image of \(\pi\) generates the local degree-four field, so its valuation occurs four times among the local Frobenius conjugates. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Slopes}(A_4)=
\{(1/4)^4,(3/4)^4\}.}
\]
This recovers the fourfold Newton slopes from the local decomposition of its CM field. The two elliptic factors contribute the six slope-1/2 roots in the full genus-seven Jacobian.


## G-33 — Artin sign character and explicit prime splitting

Write \(K^+=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\pi)\). G-31 proves
\[
\mathrm{Cl}^+(K^+)\cong C_2,
\]
and identifies \(K/K^+\) with its narrow Hilbert class field. The unit-signature subgroup is exactly the even-parity subgroup of \(\{\pm1\}^4\). Consequently the narrow class of a principal ideal \((\alpha)\) is determined by the parity of the number of negative real embeddings of \(\alpha\), equivalently by the sign of \(N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)\). Under the Artin isomorphism this parity is the Frobenius character:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Frob}_{(\alpha)}(K/K^+)=1
\iff N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)>0.}
\]
Here the identity means complete splitting in the quadratic extension; negative norm means inertness.

This gives concrete splitting checks.

- For \(p=3,5\), the quartic \(g(Y)\) is irreducible modulo \(p\), so \((p)\) is a prime ideal of norm \(p^4\). Its positive generator \(p\) makes its narrow class trivial, so it splits in \(K/K^+\). Accordingly the degree-eight Frobenius polynomial factors modulo each of 3 and 5 as two irreducible quartics.
- Modulo 7,
\[
g(Y)=Y(Y-2)(Y^2-Y+3).
\]
Let \(\mathfrak p_0=(7,\beta)\), \(\mathfrak p_2=(7,\beta-2)\), and let \(\mathfrak q\) be the degree-two prime from the quadratic factor. Since
\[
N(\beta)=N(\beta-2)=-14,\qquad N(\beta+2)=2,
\]
the ideals \((\beta)\) and \((\beta-2)\) factor as the prime above 2 times \(\mathfrak p_0\) and \(\mathfrak p_2\), respectively. The prime above 2 is narrow-principal because its generator \(\beta+2\) has even sign parity and the units realize every even-parity signature. Thus \(\mathfrak p_0\) and \(\mathfrak p_2\) represent the nontrivial narrow class and are inert in \(K/K^+\). Since \((7)=\mathfrak p_0\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak q\), the class of \(\mathfrak q\) is trivial, so \(\mathfrak q\) splits.

The degree-eight Frobenius polynomial therefore factors modulo 7 as four irreducible quadratics, matching the relative splitting pattern: two degree-one primes inert, and one degree-two prime split. The exact modular products and irreducibility checks are in `certificates/g33_cm_artin_splitting.py`.

This is a concrete Artin reciprocity law for the genus-seven simple factor's CM field. It does not yet determine the full class group of the degree-eight CM field.


## G-34 — oddness of the degree-eight CM class number

Put \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and \(L=\mathbb Q(\pi)\). The extension \(L/F\) is quadratic, unramified at every finite place, and ramified at all four real places. We know \(h_F=1\) and, from G-31, the unit-signature image has order eight.

At every finite place, an unramified local extension has surjective norm on unit groups. At each real place that becomes complex, the local norm subgroup is \(\mathbb R_{>0}\). The Hasse norm theorem therefore gives
\[
E_F\cap N_{L/F}(L^\times)=E_F^+,
\]
the totally positive units, and
\[
[E_F:E_F\cap N(L^\times)]=[E_F:E_F^+]=8.
\]

The ambiguous class number formula for a quadratic extension, counting infinite ramified places, now gives
\[
|\operatorname{Cl}(L)^{\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)}|
=h_F\frac{2^{t-1}}{[E_F:E_F\cap N(L^\times)]}
=1\cdot\frac{2^3}{8}=1.
\]
For any ideal class \(c\) of \(L\), its norm is trivial because \(h_F=1\). Hence complex conjugation sends \(c\) to \(c^{-1}\). Its fixed subgroup is precisely the 2-torsion subgroup of \(\operatorname{Cl}(L)\). Since the fixed subgroup is trivial,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(L)[2]=0,\qquad h_L\text{ is odd}.}
\]

This does not determine the odd part of the class group, nor prove that \(h_L=1\). The ambiguous class formula and its infinite-place convention are stated, for example, in Martinet's notes on units in quadratic fields, Theorem 0.1.


## G-35A — relative integral basis for the CM field

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(L=F(\pi)\), with
\[
g(\beta)=0,\qquad \pi^2-\beta\pi+2=0.
\]
Put \(\mathfrak p=(\beta+2)\). Since \(N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\beta+2)=g(-2)=2\), this is the unique prime above 2 and \((2)=\mathfrak p^4\). The G-29 local calculation gives
\[
(\beta^2-8)=\mathfrak p^2.
\]
Define
\[
\theta=\frac{\pi}{\beta+2}.
\]
Substitution into the quadratic equation gives the monic polynomial
\[
X^2-\frac{\beta}{\beta+2}X+
\frac{2}{(\beta+2)^2}.
\]
Its coefficients are integral at \(\mathfrak p\), since
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}\!\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+2}\right)=0,
\qquad
v_{\mathfrak p}\!\left(\frac{2}{(\beta+2)^2}\right)=2.
\]
At every other finite prime the denominator is a unit and \(\pi\) is integral. Thus \(\theta\) is an algebraic integer.

The relative discriminant of this monic polynomial is
\[
\frac{\beta^2-8}{(\beta+2)^2},
\]
which generates the unit ideal by the two ideal identities above. Hence the order \(\mathcal O_F[\theta]\) has unit relative discriminant. G-29 already proves that the relative field discriminant of \(L/F\) is the unit ideal. The relative order-index discriminant identity therefore forces the index
\([\mathcal O_L:\mathcal O_F[\theta]]\) to be the unit ideal. We conclude
\[
\boxed{\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_F[\theta],
\quad \theta=\pi/(\beta+2).}
\]
This corrects the use of \(\pi\) as a candidate relative integral-basis generator: the rescaled generator is the one with unit relative discriminant. It is the appropriate starting point for an exact enumeration of prime ideals under the G-35 Minkowski bound.

The order-index discriminant identity used here is the standard relation between the discriminant of an order and that of its integral closure; see, for example, MIT 18.785 Lecture 12, Theorem 12.22 and the displayed index formula.


## G-35B — both primes above 2 are principal

Reduce the G-35A monic polynomial of \(\theta=\pi/q\), where \(q=\beta+2\), modulo the unique prime \(\mathfrak p=(q)\) of \(F\) above 2. Since
\[
\frac{\beta}{q}=1-\frac2q\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p},
\qquad
\frac2{q^2}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p},
\]
the reduction is \(X^2-X=X(X-1)\). The relative integral-basis result makes this factorization exact for the maximal order, so
\[
\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L=\mathfrak P_0\mathfrak P_1,
\qquad N_{L/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak P_i)=2.
\]
The two local roots of \(X^2-\beta X+2\) have \(\mathfrak p\)-valuations 1 and 3, while \(v_{\mathfrak p}(q)=1\). Thus the corresponding values of \(\theta=\pi/q\) have valuations 0 and 2. Its absolute norm is
\[
N_{L/\mathbb Q}(\theta)
=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(2/q^2)=4.
\]
It follows that, with a suitable labeling,
\[
(\theta)=\mathfrak P_1^2.
\]
Thus \([\mathfrak P_1]\) is 2-torsion. G-34 proves \(\operatorname{Cl}(L)[2]=0\), so \(\mathfrak P_1\) is principal. Since \((q)\mathcal O_L=\mathfrak P_0\mathfrak P_1\), the other prime \(\mathfrak P_0\) is principal as well:
\[
\boxed{\text{both primes of }L\text{ above }2\text{ are principal}.}
\]
This eliminates all ideals supported only above 2 from the remaining Minkowski class-group search.


## G-35C — finite support of the remaining Minkowski classes

The degree-eight Minkowski bound is
\[
M_L=\frac{8!}{8^8}\left(\frac4\pi\right)^4
\sqrt{2^{16}17^2}<27.488.
\]
Every ideal class therefore has an integral representative of norm at most 27. The rational primes 3 and 5 are inert in \(F\), so their prime ideals already have norms \(3^4\) and \(5^4\), both above the bound. At 7, the two degree-one primes of \(F\) are inert in \(L/F\), and the degree-two prime splits in \(L/F\); all resulting prime ideals of \(L\) have norm \(7^2=49\). The primes above 2 are principal by G-35B.

For any rational prime \(p\ge11\), a prime ideal of \(L\) with residue degree at least two has norm at least \(p^2>27\). Thus the only prime ideals that can occur in a Minkowski representative and have not already been eliminated lie above
\[
\boxed{11,13,17,19,23}
\]
and have residue degree one over \(\mathbb Q\). It follows that the ideal classes of these degree-one primes generate \(\operatorname{Cl}(L)\). This is a finite support reduction, not yet a computation of their principality or relations.


## G-35D — the CM class group is cyclic

The element \(\theta=\pi/(\beta+2)\) is primitive over \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed, with \(F(X)=X^8+4X^7+6X^6+4X^5+2X^4+8X^3+24X^2+32X+16\), the degree-eight polynomial of \(\pi\), we have
\[
\theta=\frac{\pi^2}{\pi^2+2\pi+2}.
\]
For two conjugates \(x,y\), equality of these rational functions implies either \(x=y\) or \(xy+x+y=0\). The latter would make \(x\) a common root of \(F(X)\) and
\[
(X+1)^8F\!\left(-\frac{X}{X+1}\right)
=X^8+12X^7+62X^6+180X^5+322X^4
+360X^3+248X^2+96X+16.
\]
Their resultant is \(2^{44}\ne0\), so the eight conjugates of \(\theta\) are distinct. Eliminating \(\beta\) from \(g(\beta)=0\) and
\[
X^2(\beta+2)^2-\beta X(\beta+2)+2=0
\]
gives \(4H(X)\), where
\[
H(X)=X^8-8X^7+26X^6-44X^5+53X^4
-68X^3+64X^2-24X+4.
\]
Thus \(H\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\theta\). Its discriminant is \(2^{38}17^2\), while \(\operatorname{disc}(L)=2^{16}17^2\), so
\[
[\mathcal O_L:\mathbb Z[\theta]]=2^{11}.
\]
In particular this index is prime to 11, 13, 17, 19, and 23.

Exact modular factorization of \(H\) gives:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
p&\text{irreducible factor degrees, with multiplicity}\
\hline
11&4+4\\
13&2+2+2+2\\
17&1^2+1^2+2+2\\
19&2+2+2+2\\
23&2+2+2+2
\end{array}
\]
The factors and irreducibility tests are checked in certificates/g35d_cm_minkowski_primes.py. Since the index is prime to these primes, these are the actual prime-ideal residue degrees in \(\mathcal O_L\). Thus only two prime ideals \(\mathfrak P_1,\mathfrak P_2\) of norm 17 can occur in a Minkowski representative; all other prime ideals over these five rational primes have norm greater than 27.

For the real quartic, \(g(-4)=34\) and \(g(Y)\bmod17=(Y+4)^2(Y^2-4Y-3)\). Hence \((\beta+4)\) is the product of the unique prime \(\mathfrak p_2=(\beta+2)\) above 2 and the degree-one prime \(\mathfrak p_{17}=(17,\beta+4)\) above 17. Therefore
\[
\gamma=\frac{\beta+4}{\beta+2}\in\mathcal O_F,
\qquad (\gamma)=\mathfrak p_{17},
\qquad N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)=17.
\]
Its norm is positive, so its sign vector has even parity. The previously established full even-parity unit-signature subgroup lets us multiply \(\gamma\) by a unit to make it totally positive; thus \(\mathfrak p_{17}\) is narrow-principal. Since \(L/F\) is the narrow Hilbert class field, \(\mathfrak p_{17}\) splits:
\[
\mathfrak p_{17}\mathcal O_L=\mathfrak P_1\mathfrak P_2.
\]
By G-35B, all prime ideals above 2 are principal. Minkowski's bound is below 27, and the factorization table shows that every nonprincipal Minkowski representative must therefore be one of \(\mathfrak P_1,\mathfrak P_2\). Their product is principal, so their classes are inverse. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(L)=\langle[\mathfrak P_1]\rangle
\text{ is cyclic of odd order}.}
\]
At the G-35D stage the order of this cyclic group was not yet determined. G-35E below supplies an explicit generator of a norm-17 prime and completes the class-number calculation. The exact modular factorization, Rabin irreducibility checks, discriminant, index, and primitive-element resultant are in certificates/g35d_cm_minkowski_primes.py.


## G-35E — explicit norm generator and class number one

The cyclic class-group reduction G-35D leaves only the two prime ideals of norm 17. They split the extension of the unique degree-one prime \(\mathfrak p_{17}\) of \(F\), and their product is generated by
\[
\gamma=\frac{\beta+4}{\beta+2},\qquad
(\gamma)=\mathfrak p_{17},\qquad N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)=17.
\]
An explicit generator for one of the primes is obtained from
\[
q=\beta+2,\quad \theta=\pi/q,\quad
x=-1-\beta,\quad
y=-4-3\beta+2\beta^2+\beta^3,
\quad \alpha=x+y\theta.
\]
Because \(\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_F[\theta]\), this \(\alpha\) is integral. The relative norm formula is
\[
N_{L/F}(x+y\theta)
=x^2+\frac{\beta}{q}xy+\frac2{q^2}y^2.
\]
After multiplying by \(q^2\), the claimed identity is the polynomial identity in \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\)
\[
q^2x^2+\beta qxy+2y^2
=(\beta+4)(\beta+1)q.
\]
It is checked exactly by reducing with
\[
\beta^4=-4\beta^3+2\beta^2+20\beta+14.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{N_{L/F}(\alpha)=\gamma(1+\beta).}
\]
Since \(N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)=g(-4)/g(-2)=34/2=17\) and
\[
N_{F/\mathbb Q}(1+\beta)=g(-1)=1,
\]
we have \(|N_{L/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)|=17\). The integral principal ideal \((\alpha)\) therefore has prime norm 17 and is one of the two prime ideals above 17. Their product is \((\gamma)\mathcal O_L\), so the other prime is principal as well.

G-35D proves that every ideal class has a Minkowski representative supported only at the primes above 2 or these two primes above 17; the primes above 2 are principal by G-35B. Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(L)=1,\qquad h_L=1.}
\]
The exact integer-ring calculation is independently checked by the standard-library script certificates/g35e_cm_principal_generator.py.
