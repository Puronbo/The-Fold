# Stage 24 — Finite affine transformation-group recognition

## Goal

Recognize whether a finite, explicitly supplied collection of scalar affine maps forms a group under function composition.

## Mathematical model

An element is
\[
f(x)=ax+b.
\]
It is bijective on the rationals (and reals) exactly when (a\ne0). Composition is
\[
(a,b)\circ(c,d)=(ac,ad+b).
\]
The identity is ((1,0)), and for (a\ne0),
\[
(a,b)^{-1}=\left(\frac1a,-\frac ba\right).
\]

## Recognition conditions

The engine checks:
1. The identity map is present.
2. Every supplied map is bijective.
3. Every ordered composition of two supplied maps is in the collection.
4. Every supplied map's inverse is in the collection.

Associativity is not estimated from samples: it follows from associativity of function composition. Therefore, once these finite conditions hold, the set is a group.

## Proposition

A nonempty finite subset (G) of bijective affine maps is a group under composition if and only if it contains the identity and is closed under composition.

**Proof.** Associativity is inherited from function composition. Closure and finiteness imply that for every (g\in G), the sequence (g,g^2,g^3,\ldots) repeats: (g^i=g^j) for some (i<j). Since each element is bijective, cancellation gives (g^{j-i}=e), so (g^{j-i-1}) is an inverse in (G). Thus the identity and closure conditions suffice. The implementation also checks inverses explicitly as an audit trail. The converse follows directly from the group axioms. \(\square\)

## Important structural restriction

A finite subgroup of the real affine group is extremely limited: it is either the identity-only group or a two-element group generated by a reflection. This follows because the slope map (f(x)=ax+b\mapsto a) is a homomorphism into the multiplicative group of nonzero reals; the kernel consists of translations, and no nonzero translation has finite order. Finite subgroups of positive real scalings are trivial, while the only finite subgroup of real nonzero scalings is ({1,-1}). Any nontrivial finite affine subgroup is conjugate to ({x\mapsto x,\ x\mapsto -x}).

This gives the recognizer a strong mathematical sanity check: it should not discover finite groups of three or more distinct real affine maps.

## Limits

The engine recognizes only finite sets supplied by the caller and only scalar affine bijections. It does not infer an operation from raw data, discover infinite groups, classify arbitrary transformation groups, or claim AGI. A successful report certifies the declared finite set under exact arithmetic, not a broader unknown system.
