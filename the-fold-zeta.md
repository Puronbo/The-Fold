# The critical line is a crease

## The claim

The claim is that the Riemann Hypothesis is a change of scale in imaginary space: the complex plane
becomes a field, the zeta function compresses and stretches it, the critical line is the axis of
perfect symmetry, and the primes are an illusion generated by the zeros.

Part of this is exact, and the exact part is stronger than the metaphor. The Dirichlet term is the
Fold's complex `k` at height `h = ln n`. The functional equation plus reality is a fold, and the
critical line is its crease. The explicit formula is the exchange between the primes and the zeros.
What the Fold does **not** do is prove the hypothesis. It gives the crease and the dictionary; the
conjecture is that the zeros lie on the crease.

## What is exact: the Dirichlet term is the Fold's `k`

For `Re s > 1`,

```
ζ(s) = Σ_{n≥1} n^{-s} = Σ_{n≥1} n^{-σ} e^{-it ln n}
```

Each term is a complex number with modulus and argument

```
k_n = n^{-s}          |k_n| = n^{-σ}          arg k_n = −t ln n
```

This is exactly the master key: `k` is one complex number, its modulus is size, its angle is turn. The
height is

```
h = ln n
```

so the term is

```
n^{-s} = e^{-σh} e^{-ith}
```

a scale by `e^{-σh}` and a twist by `−th`. The sum over `n` is a sum over the log-lattice of heights.
The real part `σ` is the damping rate; the imaginary part `t` is the frequency. That is the exact
content of "the imaginary dimension handles rotation, the real dimension damping."

The Euler product is the other ledger:

```
ζ(s) = Π_p (1 − p^{-s})^{-1},          log ζ(s) = Σ_p Σ_{m≥1} (1/m) p^{-ms}
```

The primes are the multiplicative generators; the integers are the free commutative monoid on them.
The Dirichlet series is additive; the Euler product is multiplicative. The two ledgers of the Fold
appear here as the two representations of the same function, and the explicit formula is the exchange
rate.

Two corrections of language. `ℂ` is a field in the algebraic sense; it is not a physical field, and
the zeta function is holomorphic, not a medium with neighbor interactions. The imaginary axis is a
phase axis, not a spatial dimension. The geometry is exact; the physics words are not.

## The functional equation is a fold

Complete the zeta function:

```
ξ(s) = ½ s(s−1) π^{−s/2} Γ(s/2) ζ(s)
```

`ξ` is entire, and the Gamma poles cancel the trivial zeros of `ζ`; the zeros of `ξ` are exactly the
nontrivial zeros. It satisfies

```
ξ(s) = ξ(1−s)                  ξ(s̄) = conj ξ(s)
```

The first is the functional equation: a half-turn about `s = 1/2`. The second is reality: a reflection
across the real axis. Their composition is the reflection across the critical line:

```
R(s) = 1 − s̄
```

In the coordinate `s = 1/2 + z`, this is `z ↦ −z̄`: a reflection with crease `Re z = 0`, i.e.
`Re s = 1/2`. It is an involution, it reverses orientation, and it fixes the critical line pointwise —
a fold in the exact sense of `σ_H`. The midpoint of `s` and `R(s)` is `1/2 + i Im s`, on the crease;
the projection `π_H = ½(I + R)` is the critical-line projection, the same midpoint map as the Fold's.

The consequences are exact:

```
ξ(R(s)) = conj ξ(s)          |ξ(R(s))| = |ξ(s)|
```

So the modulus is invariant under the fold, and the zero set is symmetric under it. If `ρ` is a zero,
then `R(ρ)` is a zero. The hypothesis is that the two coincide:

```
RH  ⇔  every zero of ξ is fixed by R  ⇔  every zero lies on the crease Re s = 1/2
```

This is a reformulation, not a proof. The functional equation alone pairs `ρ` with `1−ρ`; it does not
force the pair onto the crease. Reality adds the conjugate `ρ̄`; the composition gives the reflection
`R`; but a symmetric pair can still sit off the crease, at `ρ` and `R(ρ)`. RH says that pair is
trivial. The Fold explains why a crease is special; it does not force these zeros onto it.

One warning about the phrase "the critical line is the fixed set of the functional equation." It is
not: the fixed set of `s ↦ 1−s` is the single point `s = 1/2`. The critical line is the fixed set of
the **composition** of the functional equation with reality, i.e. of `R`. That composition is the
fold.

## The explicit formula is the exchange

For `x > 1`, with the zeros paired,

```
ψ(x) = x − Σ_ρ x^ρ/ρ − ½ ln(1 − x^{−2}) − ln(2π)
```

where `ψ(x) = Σ_{p^k ≤ x} ln p`. This is the exact version of "the zeros are the harmonics of the
primes": the zeros are the frequencies, the primes are the events, and the formula is the Fourier
exchange between them. The primes are not an illusion generated by the zeros. The Euler product
defines `ζ` from the primes; the zeros are its spectrum; the explicit formula recovers the primes from
the spectrum. Each determines the other. The apparent randomness is the additive view; the
multiplicative view is unique factorization.

## The Fold dictionary

| Fold | zeta | status |
|---|---|---|
| complex `k` | `n^{-s}` | exact: `\|k\| = n^{−σ}`, `arg k = −t ln n` |
| height `h` | `ln n` | exact |
| scale `\|k\|` | `e^{−σh}` | exact |
| twist `arg k` | `−th` | exact |
| datum / point | `0` / `1` | exact for the term |
| fold `σ_H` | `R(s) = 1 − s̄` | exact reflection; crease `Re s = 1/2` |

| zero on the crease | RH zero | exact reformulation; RH open |
| fold ledger | Dirichlet series, explicit formula | reading |

The last two rows are warnings. The pole at `s = 1` is the edge of the Euler product's convergence,
not a null surface; the Fold's horizon is a null crease, and the analogy does not carry. The
signature `(0,0,n)` is the inertia of the zero map; a zero of `ξ` is a cancellation point of a
function, not a collapse of the tangent space. The signature result does not apply to zeta zeros.

## What is not exact

- **"The complex plane is a dynamical field."** It is the domain of a holomorphic function. `ℂ` is a
  field in the algebraic sense; the physical sense is a metaphor.
- **"The imaginary dimension is a dimension."** It is the phase/frequency axis. The amplitude is
  `n^{−σ}`; the phase is `−t ln n`. Moving `t` changes the frequency, not the magnification.
- **"The critical line is an equilibrium of forces."** It is the crease of the reflection `R`. The
  only exact balance is the symmetry `ξ(s) = ξ(1−s)` and `|ξ(R(s))| = |ξ(s)|`.
- **"The primes are an illusion generated by the zeros."** They are dual, not generated one way. The
  Euler product and the explicit formula are two directions of the same exchange.
- **"The zeros are where space collapses to zero."** A zero of `ζ` is a cancellation of the sum, not
  the zero map. The value is zero; the operator is not.
- **"RH is a change of scale."** The change of scale is the term `n^{-s}`. RH is the location of the
  zeros on the crease. The two are related by the dictionary, not identical.
- **"The Fold proves RH."** It does not. The functional equation gives the crease and pairs the zeros;
  it does not eliminate off-crease pairs. That elimination is the open problem.

## What this does and does not say

- It **does** identify the Dirichlet term with the Fold's complex `k`: `n^{-s}` is a scale by
  `n^{−σ}` and a twist by `−t ln n` at height `ln n`.
- It **does** identify the functional equation with a fold: `R(s) = 1 − s̄` is a reflection whose
  crease is the critical line.
- It **does** restate RH exactly: every zero of `ξ` is fixed by `R`.
- It **does** identify the explicit formula as the exchange between the multiplicative and additive
  ledgers.
- It **does not** prove RH. Symmetry gives the crease and pairs the zeros; it does not force the pairs
  onto the crease.
- It **does not** make the zeros a Fold zero. A zero of a function is not the zero map; `(0,0,n)` does
  not apply.
- It **does not** make the primes an illusion. The primes and the zeros are dual.

## The one sentence

**The critical line is the crease of the completed symmetry — functional equation plus reality; RH
says the zeros lie on the crease. The Dirichlet series is the Fold's scale-and-twist sum, the primes
are the generators, the zeros are the harmonics — and the dictionary is not a proof.**
